# The seven pillars of friendship

> **TL;DR.** Dunbar's group found friendship closeness tracks how many of **seven "pillars"** two people share — language, upbringing, education/career, hobbies, worldview, humour, and musical taste. The more you share, the closer and more resilient the bond. It's homophily ("birds of a feather") made specific.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

The **seven pillars** — shared dimensions that predict friendship strength:

1. Same **language** / dialect
2. Growing up in the same **place**
3. Same **education / career** path
4. Same **hobbies / interests**
5. Same **worldview** (moral, religious, political)
6. Same **sense of humour**
7. Same **musical taste**

Closeness roughly scales with the *number* of pillars shared; they also define the *kind* of friend (a gym friend vs. a friend who shares your worldview). This is the mechanism beneath the broader finding that **similarity** drives liking (see [similarity & mere exposure](../connection/similarity-and-mere-exposure.md)).

!!! note "Descriptive, not a checklist"
    The pillars *predict* closeness on average; they aren't requirements, and shared adversity or sheer time can build deep friendships across big differences.

## How to apply

- **To deepen a friendship, find and grow the shared pillars** — a common hobby or a real worldview conversation does more than generic hangout time.
- **To read a friendship,** notice *which* pillars you share: it explains why some friends are situational and others feel "for life."
