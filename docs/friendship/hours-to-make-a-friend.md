# Hours to make a friend

> **TL;DR.** Closeness runs on **accumulated hours together**: ~**50** hours to turn an acquaintance into a casual friend, ~**90** to a "real" friend, and **200+** to a close friend. Time is *necessary but not sufficient* — it has to be the right kind of time.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Jeffrey Hall (2019) estimated the hours of shared time behind each step up in closeness:

- **~50 hours** (40–60) → acquaintance becomes a **casual friend**
- **~90 hours** (80–100) → a **friend**
- **200+ hours** → a **close / best friend**

Two caveats from the work itself: the estimates come from **self-report / recall** with a modest sample, and **hours alone don't do it** — *obligatory* time (e.g., colleagues forced together) can even correlate *negatively*. What converts time into closeness is **shared activity, play, and meaningful everyday talk**, not mere co-presence.

## How to apply

- **Front-load frequency with new friends.** Hours add up through *repeated* contact; a burst early (a trip, a project, a standing weekly thing) crosses thresholds faster than occasional hangs.
- **Make the hours count.** Catch-ups and real self-disclosure (see [the 36 questions](../connection/the-36-questions.md)) move the needle; parallel small talk mostly doesn't.
- **Adults underestimate this.** Friendships stop "just happening" after school because the default shared hours vanish — you now have to manufacture them deliberately.
