# Sleep: the core

> **TL;DR.** Sleep is the **single highest-ROI health lever** — the maintenance window for memory, metabolism, hormones, immune function, and brain waste-clearance. The mainstream consensus is simple and robust: **most adults need 7–9 hours**, and chronic short sleep degrades nearly everything. (The popular book *Why We Sleep* oversells some specifics — lean on the consensus, not the scariest claims.)

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-08

## What it is

Sleep isn't downtime; it's active maintenance. During it the brain **consolidates memory** (the [learning](../learning/index.md) payoff), the **glymphatic system clears metabolic waste** including amyloid-β (Xie et al., 2013), and hormonal/metabolic and immune systems reset. Chronic short sleep is associated with worse cognition, mood, metabolic and cardiovascular risk, and a U-shaped mortality curve.

The numbers worth anchoring on come from consensus panels, not any one author: the American Academy of Sleep Medicine & Sleep Research Society conclude adults should sleep **≥7 hours** regularly (Watson et al., 2015); the National Sleep Foundation recommends **7–9 hours** (Hirshkowitz et al., 2015).

!!! warning "Contested — *Why We Sleep* overreaches"
    Matthew Walker's *Why We Sleep* (2017) is the popular anchor but contains documented factual errors and overstated claims — catalogued at length by Alexey Guzey (2019), with some points Walker later acknowledged. The **core message (sleep matters; aim 7–9h) is solid consensus.** Treat Walker's strongest specific assertions (deterministic "short sleep → early death," sweeping single-cause disease claims) skeptically, and cite consensus statements rather than the book.
