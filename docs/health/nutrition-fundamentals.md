# Nutrition fundamentals

> **TL;DR.** Nutrition science is noisy and over-reported, but a **small core is robust**: eat mostly **minimally-processed whole foods**, get **enough protein and fiber**, and remember **energy balance governs weight**. The biggest evidence-backed lever for most people is **cutting ultra-processed food** — which drives spontaneous overeating. Almost everything past that core is marginal or contested; ignore the churn and the supplements.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high (core) / contested (margins) · **Updated:** 2026-06-08

## What it is

The few things that survive scrutiny:

- **Ultra-processed foods (UPFs) drive overeating.** In Hall et al.'s (2019) tightly-controlled inpatient RCT — matched meals for calories, sugar, fat, fiber — people ate **~500 kcal/day more** and gained weight on the ultra-processed diet vs. the unprocessed one, *without being told to eat more*. Strong, mechanistic evidence that processing itself matters, not just macros.
- **Protein adequacy** supports muscle, satiety, and body composition (training detail → [protein & nutrition](../gym/protein-and-nutrition.md)).
- **Fiber / plants** track with better metabolic and gut outcomes.
- **Energy balance** still sets weight; "whole foods" mostly works by making overeating harder.

!!! warning "Contested at the margins"
    Most diet headlines come from **observational** studies (confounded, weak effects) or short trials. Specific "superfoods," exact macro ratios, meal timing, and most supplements are **noise or genuinely disputed.** Anchor on the robust core above; treat the rest as low-priority and provisional.

## How to apply

- **Base meals on whole foods + protein + fiber;** minimize ultra-processed products (the single highest-yield change).
- **Don't chase supplements over basics** — same lesson as [the unsexy basics](../lookism/the-unsexy-basics.md): boring fundamentals, done consistently.
- **For weight, manage energy balance** via food choice and protein, not willpower at the table.
