# Sleep regularity & circadian rhythm

> **TL;DR.** *When* and *how consistently* you sleep may matter as much as *how long*. Irregular sleep timing predicts worse health — in a large study, **sleep regularity beat sleep duration** as a mortality predictor. The body runs on a ~24h clock entrained mainly by **light**, so a regular schedule plus morning daylight and a dark evening is most of the game.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-08

## What it is

Your master clock (the suprachiasmatic nucleus) is set primarily by **light**: morning bright light anchors the clock and your alertness rhythm; evening light — especially blue — **delays the clock and suppresses melatonin**, pushing sleep later. Living against this rhythm ("**social jetlag**," Roenneberg — the gap between weekday and weekend sleep timing) tracks with worse metabolic and mental-health outcomes.

The striking recent finding: analysing accelerometer data from ~60,000 UK Biobank participants, **Windred et al. (2024) found sleep *regularity* was a stronger predictor of all-cause, cardiometabolic, and cancer mortality than sleep *duration*.** Going to bed and waking at consistent times appears to matter more than hitting an exact hour count — a counterintuitive, high-leverage result.

## How to apply

- **Fix the wake time first,** seven days a week — a consistent rise time anchors the whole rhythm (weekend lie-ins re-create jet lag).
- **Get bright light early** (outdoors beats any indoor light), and **dim/avoid screens at night**; keep the bedroom dark and cool.
- **Hold a regular schedule even when total sleep varies** — consistency is the lever, not perfection on duration alone.
