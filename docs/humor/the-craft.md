# The craft: timing, structure & devices

> **TL;DR.** Jokes are *built*, not born. The machinery: a **setup** that primes one expectation and a **punchline** that snaps to another (incongruity), delivered with **timing** and **brevity** — plus repeatable devices: the rule of three, misdirection, callbacks, exaggeration, the act-out.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-06

## What it is

**Structure.** Most jokes are setup → punchline: the setup builds an assumption; the punchline reveals a second, surprising-but-fitting reading. The bigger and *faster* the reframe, the bigger the laugh.

**Timing & brevity.** *"Brevity is the soul of wit"* — every extra word leaks tension. Comedy lives in the **edit** and the **pause**: land the surprising word **last**, then stop. A beat of silence before the punch sets it; rushing kills it.

**The core devices:**

- **Rule of three** — two items set a pattern, the third breaks it (the surprise rides the pattern).
- **Misdirection** — lead the listener confidently the wrong way, then pivot.
- **Callbacks** — re-reference an earlier joke later; the payoff feels earned and *shared* — you've built an inside joke in real time.
- **Exaggeration & specificity** — push it absurdly far, or get oddly *specific* (specific is funnier than general).
- **The act-out** — *show* the moment (voice, face) instead of describing it.

## How to apply

- **Put the funny word last, then shut up.** Trim every joke to its shortest form.
- **Plant for callbacks** — note an early laugh and return to it later.
- **Be specific and commit** — a hedged, half-said joke isn't a joke.
