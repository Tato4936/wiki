# Benign violation theory

> **TL;DR.** The best modern account of *what makes things funny* (McGraw & Warren): humor happens when something is **a violation** (wrong, threatening, norm-breaking) **and simultaneously benign** (safe, playful, okay). Too benign → boring; too violating → offensive. The laugh lives in the overlap.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-06

## What it is

A *violation* is anything that threatens how you think the world *should* be — a broken norm, a taboo, a logical wrong, a mild misfortune. It's *benign* when something **simultaneously** signals "but it's safe / not serious": you're playing, it isn't real, it's far away, another norm says it's fine. **Humor needs both at once.**

This explains the things people half-know:

- **Comedic distance** — "comedy = tragedy + time," or *far away*. A pratfall is funny to a bystander (benign) but painful to the victim (pure violation); time and distance turn a violation benign.
- **The two ways jokes die:** too tame (no violation — the pun everyone saw coming) *or* too far (a violation no framing makes safe — the "too soon" joke).
- **Why *who* and *where* matter:** the same line is benign among close friends (trust frames it as play) and a violation among strangers.

## How to apply

- **To be funnier, add a small violation** to the merely pleasant — a mild transgression, an unexpected wrong, a taboo lightly touched.
- **To not bomb, keep it benign** — signal play (tone, smile, self-targeting), read the trust in the room, respect distance ("too soon").
- **Diagnose a flat joke:** no violation (boring) or not benign (offensive)? Fix the missing half.
