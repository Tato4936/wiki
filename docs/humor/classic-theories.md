# The classic theories

> **TL;DR.** Three old theories each capture *part* of humor: **incongruity** (funny = a surprise that breaks expectation), **superiority** (we laugh at others' folly/misfortune), and **relief** (laughter releases tension). None is complete alone — which is why [benign violation](benign-violation-theory.md) was proposed to unify them.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-06

## What it is

- **Incongruity** (Kant, Schopenhauer): humor comes from a **violated expectation** — the setup primes one reading, the punchline forces another. The dominant cognitive account; explains misdirection, the rule of three, wordplay. *Limit:* not every surprise is funny — some are just confusing or frightening.
- **Superiority** (Hobbes' "sudden glory"; Plato): we laugh from a sense of **superiority** over others' (or our own past) flaws. Explains mockery, schadenfreude, much teasing. *Limit:* plenty of humor has no victim at all.
- **Relief** (Freud, Spencer): laughter **discharges** built-up nervous or psychic tension (taboo, anxiety). Explains nervous laughter and why taboo topics are comic fuel. *Limit:* doesn't say *why a particular thing* is funny.

!!! note "They're routes into the sweet spot"
    Each really describes a way *into* [benign violation](benign-violation-theory.md): incongruity supplies the **violation** of expectation; superiority and relief describe contexts that keep it **benign** ("it's them, not me"; "it's only a joke"). Use them as a toolkit of *ways to be funny*, not rival dogmas.

## How to apply

- **Incongruity** → engineer surprise: set an expectation, then break it (see [the craft](the-craft.md)).
- **Superiority** → keep targets safe: punch *up* or at *yourself*, never down (see [self-deprecation](self-deprecation.md)).
- **Relief** → name the tension everyone's feeling; the taboo lightly touched gets the biggest laugh.
