# Humor styles

> **TL;DR.** Martin's research splits humor into **four styles** on two axes (toward self vs. others × benign vs. harmful). Two **build** wellbeing and bonds — **affiliative** and **self-enhancing**; two **harm** them — **aggressive** and **self-defeating**. *Which* humor you use matters more than how much.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-06

## What it is

From the **Humor Styles Questionnaire** (Martin et al., 2003):

| | **Benign / adaptive** | **Harmful / maladaptive** |
|---|---|---|
| **Toward others** | **Affiliative** — playful, inclusive joking that pulls people together | **Aggressive** — sarcasm, ridicule, put-downs at others' expense |
| **Toward self** | **Self-enhancing** — a humorous outlook that helps *you* cope with stress | **Self-defeating** — excessive self-mockery to win approval; corrosive |

The adaptive two track higher wellbeing, better relationships, and likeability; the maladaptive two track worse relationships (and, for self-defeating, lower self-esteem). Aggressive humor can get *laughs* while *costing* closeness — the laugh is real, the bond is the price.

## How to apply

- **Cultivate affiliative** (joke *with*, include, riff on the shared situation) and **self-enhancing** (find the funny in your own setbacks).
- **Audit your default:** if your go-to is sarcasm/teasing-with-an-edge (aggressive) or constant self-put-downs (self-defeating), that's a fixable habit quietly eroding your relationships.
- **The test:** does the joke pull people *together* (adaptive), or create a winner and a loser (often aggressive)?
