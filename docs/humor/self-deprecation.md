# Self-deprecation

> **TL;DR.** Targeting *yourself* is the safest, most likeable way to be funny — it keeps a joke [benign](benign-violation-theory.md) (no one else is hurt) and signals confidence + humility. But there's a line: **light** self-deprecation = warm and disarming; **constant** self-deprecation = the harmful *self-defeating* style that reads as insecurity.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-06

## What it is

Who a joke targets decides whether it bonds or bruises:

- **Punching down** (at people with less power/status) reads as cruelty — the [aggressive style](humor-styles.md). Avoid it.
- **Punching up** (at the powerful, at norms, at yourself) stays benign and likeable.
- **Self-deprecation** is the most reliable: you're the only "victim," so it's automatically safe, and lightly mocking your own flaws signals you're **secure enough not to take yourself too seriously** (cousin of the [pratfall effect](../connection/the-pratfall-effect.md) — a small, humanizing flaw can *increase* liking).

The catch (Martin's **self-defeating** style): if the self-mockery is *constant* or *fishing for reassurance*, it flips from confident to insecure — and people find it uncomfortable, not endearing.

## How to apply

- **Self-deprecate from strength** — joke about a flaw you're clearly at peace with, not a raw insecurity you want soothed.
- **Punch up or at yourself; never down.**
- **Use it to defuse** status/tension (when arriving, apologizing, being praised) — a light self-jab makes you approachable.
