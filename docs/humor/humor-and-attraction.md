# Humor & attraction

> **TL;DR.** "Good sense of humor" tops mate-preference lists — with a twist: it's largely about *producing* humor being attractive, and *appreciating* it (a receptive audience) being what producers want. Humor likely works as an **honest signal** of intelligence and shared worldview.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium (evo-psych — interpret cautiously) · **Updated:** 2026-06-06

## What it is

Two leading (and speculative) accounts:

- **Fitness / intelligence signal** (Miller, *The Mating Mind*): producing good humour is *hard* — it needs quick intelligence, language, and social calibration — so it's a costly, hard-to-fake signal of underlying quality. Humour does correlate with intelligence.
- **Interest / encryption indicator** (Flamson & Barrett): a joke only lands if you *share the background knowledge* it relies on — so getting each other's humour is live evidence of **shared experience and worldview**, an instant compatibility test.

The well-replicated asymmetry (Bressler, Martin & Balshine, 2006): on average, people seeking a partner value a humour ***producer***, while producers value a *receptive audience* — "is funny" and "laughs at my jokes" are different, complementary preferences.

!!! warning "Hold this loosely"
    This is evolutionary-psychology territory — plausible and partly supported, but the just-so-story risk is real. Treat it as an interesting lens, not settled fact.

## How to apply

- **Humour is a two-way compatibility test** — getting each other's jokes is real signal about shared worldview; a mismatch is information too.
- **Don't perform "funny" *at* someone** — being a warm, genuine *audience* is at least as attractive as producing, and far less try-hard.
