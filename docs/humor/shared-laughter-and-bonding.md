# Shared laughter & bonding

> **TL;DR.** Laughter is mostly a **social** signal, not a response to jokes (Provine): we laugh far more with others than alone, and most laughter punctuates ordinary conversation, not punchlines. Its job is **bonding** — which means *appreciating* humor (laughing easily, genuinely) builds closeness as much as *producing* it.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-06

## What it is

Provine's research: laughter is **contagious**, overwhelmingly **social** (rare when truly alone), and mostly tied to *connection* — "Hey, where've you been?" draws more laughs than most actual jokes. It likely evolved as a **play signal** meaning "this is safe, we're okay" — i.e. the *benign* half of [benign violation](benign-violation-theory.md), broadcast to others.

What most people miss:

- **Being a great audience is half the skill.** People bond with whoever finds *them* funny; easy, genuine laughter is a gift that makes others feel witty and liked.
- **Genuine vs. polite laughter.** Spontaneous (Duchenne) laughter signals real connection; forced/polite laughter signals distance — and people can tell. Aim to create the real kind, not extract the polite kind.
- **Inside jokes & callbacks are bonding machines** — a shared private reference is a tiny monument to "we have a history." Shared humour is one of the [seven pillars of friendship](../friendship/seven-pillars-of-friendship.md).

## How to apply

- **Laugh generously and genuinely** — appreciation is a connection skill, not a consolation prize for the un-funny.
- **Build inside jokes** (callbacks to shared moments); they compound across a relationship.
- **Use humour to signal safety early** — light play says "I'm not a threat."
