# Vocal tone (paralinguistics)

> **TL;DR.** *How* you say something — pitch, pace, volume, warmth, pauses — carries a huge amount of the
> emotional message, often more than the words themselves. It's the legitimate core that the
> [93% myth](the-93-percent-myth.md) distorts: tone reliably conveys feeling.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

**Paralinguistics** (or prosody) is everything vocal except the words: pitch, intonation, loudness, tempo,
rhythm, pauses, voice quality. Reviews (Scherer) show listeners decode emotion from voice fairly accurately
and fairly universally — a warm, slower, lower-pitched voice reads as calm and trustworthy; a fast, high,
tight voice reads as anxious; a flat voice reads as cold or bored, *regardless of the words*.

This is the **real signal** behind the misquoted "38% tone": not a universal percentage, but the genuine
fact that **emotional meaning rides largely on delivery.**

## How to apply

- On anything emotional or persuasive, **mind the delivery as much as the script**: slow down, drop the
  pitch a little, add warmth and pauses. The same sentence can land as caring or dismissive.
- **Match tone to message.** Reassuring words in a tense, clipped voice get read as the tone, not the words
  (see [the 93% myth](the-93-percent-myth.md)).
- On the phone, where it's *all* you've got, tone does the entire job.
