# Nonverbal synchrony (mirroring)

> **TL;DR.** We unconsciously copy the posture, gestures, and mannerisms of people we're connecting with —
> and doing it (subtly) *increases* liking and smoothness. Matching someone's body, not just their words,
> is a real rapport builder.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Chartrand & Bargh's **"chameleon effect"**: people automatically mimic each other's postures, gestures, and
expressions, and this mimicry is both a *sign* of rapport and a *cause* of it — participants whose movements
were subtly mirrored liked the other person more and found the interaction smoother. Synchrony signals
"we're in tune," mostly below awareness.

This is the **postural, behavioral cousin** of [verbal mirroring](../connection/mirroring.md): one matches
words, the other matches the body.

## How to apply

- **Subtly match** the other person's energy and posture — lean when they lean, match their pace and tone.
  Emphasis on *subtle*: deliberate, obvious copying reads as mocking and kills it.
- Often you don't need to *do* anything — just being genuinely engaged produces natural synchrony. Notice
  when it's *absent* (you're stiff and mismatched) and loosen up.
- Quick rapport check: shift your posture and see if they drift to follow. If they do, you're in sync.
