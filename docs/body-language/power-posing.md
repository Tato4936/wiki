# Power posing

> **TL;DR.** The viral claim that standing in an expansive "power pose" for two minutes raises
> testosterone, lowers cortisol, and makes you act bolder **largely failed to replicate** — one of the
> original authors publicly walked it back. You might *feel* a little more confident; your hormones and
> behavior probably don't change.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** contested · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Carney, Cuddy & Yap (2010) reported that briefly holding expansive postures produced hormonal changes and
more risk-taking — popularized by one of the most-viewed TED talks ever.

!!! warning "Contested — the big claims didn't hold"
    A larger replication (Ranehill et al., 2015) found **no hormonal or behavioral effects**, and in 2016
    the first author, Dana Carney, stated she no longer believed the effect was real. What has *more*
    support is a modest **subjective** effect — people may *report feeling* more powerful — but the headline
    physiological and behavioral claims are not reliable.

## How to apply

- Don't rely on a "power pose" to change your hormones or performance — that part isn't real.
- If standing tall before something stressful makes *you feel* steadier, fine — use it as a small
  self-signal, not a magic intervention. Real presence comes from
  [warmth](../connection/lead-with-warmth.md) and preparation, not a two-minute pose.
