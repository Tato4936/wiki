# Eye contact

> **TL;DR.** Eye contact signals attention, confidence, and intimacy — and mutual gaze can actually
> *intensify* feelings of connection. But it's a dial, not a switch: too little reads as evasive or
> insecure, too much as aggressive or intense.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Gaze is one of the most powerful nonverbal channels (Kleinke's review). Appropriate eye contact conveys
attentiveness, confidence, and liking; mutual gaze is part of how intimacy is signaled and built —
Kellerman and colleagues found that **sustained mutual eye contact between strangers increased reported
feelings of affection** (the same lever behind the [36 questions'](../connection/the-36-questions.md)
closing eye-contact step).

But it's calibrated: in most Western norms, **listeners hold more eye contact than speakers**, and the
"right" amount is culture-dependent — normal in some cultures, intrusive in others.

## How to apply

- **Hold more eye contact while listening than while talking** — it signals your focus is on them.
- Aim for **soft, comfortable** contact with natural breaks, not an unblinking stare (which reads as
  intense or hostile). The break-and-return rhythm is what feels warm.
- For closeness, a little *more* mutual gaze than usual (with someone receptive) deepens the sense of
  connection.
