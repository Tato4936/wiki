# Distribution & sales

> **TL;DR.** *"A superior product is not enough."* Thiel argues **poor distribution — not a bad product — is the #1 cause of failure.** Engineers underrate sales because good selling is *designed to look effortless*. You need a concrete way to reach and convert customers, with the unit economics to do it profitably.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-06

## What it is

"If you build it, they will come" is false — a great product with no distribution loses to a worse one that reaches people. Two ideas:

- **The power law of distribution:** usually *one* channel works for a given business; most fail. Find and dominate it instead of dabbling in all.
- **The sales spectrum** scales with deal size: **complex sales** (huge, CEO-led) → **personal sales** → **marketing/advertising** (small-ticket) → **viral** (≈zero cost per user). The **"dead zone"** — products too pricey to go viral but too cheap to justify a salesforce — is where companies quietly die.

The discipline check: **customer acquisition cost (CAC) < customer lifetime value (CLV)**, with margin to spare.

## How to apply

- **Treat distribution as a first-class design problem**, equal to the product: "how will the next 1,000 — then 1,000,000 — users actually find and buy this?"
- **Find your one channel** and go deep before diversifying.
- **Everybody sells** — founders especially. Disdaining sales is a tell of a doomed "great product will sell itself" mindset.
