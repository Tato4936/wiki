# Network effects & increasing returns

> **TL;DR.** Some products get **more valuable as more people use them** (phones, marketplaces, social networks, standards). That creates **increasing returns** — winners win harder — and tends toward **winner-take-all.** It's one of the strongest, most durable moats, and the economic engine under "software eats the world."

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-06

## What it is

- **Network effects:** each new user raises the value for existing users. **Metcalfe's law** captures the flavor — a network's value scales roughly with the *square* of its users (n²) — so value pulls ahead of linear growth.
- **Increasing returns** (Brian Arthur): unlike traditional industries with *diminishing* returns, knowledge/network industries show *positive feedback* — early leads compound (network effects, learning curves, lock-in/standards) and often **tip** the market to a single winner.

This is why platforms are prized — and hard to dislodge once they tip. Thiel's caution: network effects must be real **from small scale up** — a product only valuable once "everyone" uses it has a brutal cold-start problem (hence [start small](start-small-and-monopolize.md): win a dense niche first).

## How to apply

- **Look for (or design) network effects** — they convert early traction into a durable [moat](moats.md).
- **Solve the cold start in a small, dense market** where even a few users create real value for each other, then expand.
- In winner-take-all markets, weight **tipping**: being #1 vs. #3 is the whole game.
