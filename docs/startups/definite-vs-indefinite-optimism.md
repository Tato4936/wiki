# Definite vs. indefinite optimism

> **TL;DR.** Thiel's 2×2 of how people see the future: **definite vs. indefinite** (do you have a specific plan, or just optimize process?) × **optimism vs. pessimism**. He argues the West drifted from *definite* optimism (bold, planned futures) to *indefinite* optimism (iterate, keep options open, trust process/finance) — and that **definite beats indefinite.** This is the root of his fight with the **Lean Startup**.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-06

## What it is

The four stances:

- **Definite optimism** — future is better *and you have a concrete plan to build it* (mid-century US; bold engineering).
- **Indefinite optimism** — future is better but *you don't know how*, so keep options open, iterate, optimize process (his read of the US since ~1982: finance, "lean," consulting).
- **Definite pessimism** — bleak but plannable (his caricature of China).
- **Indefinite pessimism** — bleak and directionless (his caricature of Europe).

Thiel champions **definite optimism**: a great company is a *definite plan* to build a specific future, not a lottery ticket you iterate toward.

!!! warning "Contested — the Lean Startup disagrees"
    Eric Ries's **Lean Startup** (build–measure–learn, MVPs, pivots, "validated learning") is essentially *indefinite*: assume you can't know the answer up front, so iterate toward it cheaply. Thiel thinks this overrates iteration and underrates **vision**. Honest synthesis: **lean experimentation fits genuine demand uncertainty** (new consumer behavior); **definite planning fits deep-tech / capital-intensive bets** where you must commit before you can "test." Most strong founders do both — a bold thesis, with disciplined tests of its riskiest assumptions inside it. Pick by *how knowable your answer actually is.*

## How to apply

- **Hold a definite thesis *and* cheap tests of its riskiest assumptions** — don't confuse endless pivoting with progress, or a rigid plan with conviction.
- Ask whether your domain rewards **planning** (commit early) or **iteration** (learn cheaply), and weight accordingly.
