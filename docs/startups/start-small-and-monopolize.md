# Start small and monopolize

> **TL;DR.** The paradox: to build something huge, **start with a market small enough to dominate.** Own ~100% of a tiny niche, then expand into adjacent markets from strength. Chasing 1% of a giant "$X-billion market" usually means owning nothing.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-06

## What it is

Thiel: *"Every startup is small at the start. Every monopoly dominates a large share of its market. Therefore, every startup should start with a very small market — even too small."* Win a beachhead where you can be the obvious leader, then move outward in concentric circles.

Canonical examples: **PayPal** → eBay power-sellers; **Amazon** → books; **Facebook** → one campus. Each *monopolized a niche* first, then expanded.

This converges with Geoffrey Moore's **Crossing the Chasm**: pick one beachhead segment, dominate it, and use that to cross the gap from early adopters to the mainstream — rather than diffusely chasing everyone at once.

## How to apply

- **Define the smallest market you can plausibly own**, plus a concentrated group who badly need you — not a giant TAM you'd hold a sliver of.
- **Sequence your expansion** into adjacent segments; don't leap straight to the broad market.
- Be suspicious of any plan whose whole story is "the market is enormous, we just need 1%."
