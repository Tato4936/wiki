# Secrets & the contrarian question

> **TL;DR.** Thiel's interview question and thesis: **"What important truth do very few people agree with you on?"** Great companies are built on a **secret** — a valuable truth others don't yet see. Believing that worthwhile secrets *still exist* (vs. "everything important is already known") is what separates founders from the crowd.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-06

## What it is

A great business answers the contrarian question: it rests on a truth that's **important *and* non-consensus** ("most believe X; the truth is the opposite"). Consensus ideas are already priced in; only a secret gives room to build a monopoly before others notice.

Thiel splits secrets into **secrets about nature** (undiscovered facts about the world) and **secrets about people** (things people don't know about themselves, or won't admit). He argues culture stopped *looking* for secrets — out of complacency, risk-aversion, "flatness" (assuming a global crowd already found everything), and incrementalism — which is exactly why opportunities remain.

!!! note "Contrarian ≠ merely opposite"
    The point isn't reflexive contrarianism; it's being **right about something important that others are wrong about.** Most contrarian takes are simply wrong — the value is in the rare *true-and-non-consensus* belief.

## How to apply

- **Interrogate your thesis:** can you state the important truth *few people agree with you on*? If your idea is consensus, the upside is probably already competed away.
- **Look where others aren't** — unsexy markets, taboo problems, "everyone knows" claims that might be false.
- Use it as a filter for ideas, hires, and bets.
