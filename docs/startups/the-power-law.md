# The power law

> **TL;DR.** Startup (and venture, and arguably career) outcomes follow a **power law**, not a bell curve: a tiny number of winners hold almost all the value. The best investment in a fund can **outperform the entire rest of the fund combined.** Lesson: a few decisions dominate — **concentrate** on getting those right; don't diversify into mediocrity.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-06

## What it is

Thiel: *"the biggest secret in venture capital is that the best investment in a successful fund equals or outperforms the entire rest of the fund combined."* Returns are radically skewed — most startups → ~0, a few → enormous — and the shape recurs wherever success compounds.

Counterintuitive consequences:

- **Diversification is a trap under a power law.** Spreading thin guarantees you mostly own the losers; you must back only things that *could* be huge.
- **The single biggest choice dominates** everything after it — which company to start/join/build is worth more deliberation than years of later optimization.
- It contradicts the "lots of small hedged bets" instinct — the power law rewards **concentrated conviction**.

## How to apply

- **Pick the one big thing carefully** (company, role, market); it likely outweighs all the downstream tuning.
- **Concentrate, then commit** — don't assemble a portfolio of half-hearted options hoping one pays off.
- Distrust advice calibrated for *normal* distributions (incrementalism, broad diversification) in domains that are actually power-law.
