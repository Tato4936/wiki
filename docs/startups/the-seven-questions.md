# The seven questions

> **TL;DR.** Thiel's checklist: every great business needs a good answer to **all seven**. Most failures flunk several — usually the unglamorous ones. It's the most directly usable artifact in *Zero to One*.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-06

## What it is

1. **Engineering** — can you create *breakthrough* technology (≈10× better), not incremental gains?
2. **Timing** — is now the right time for *this* business?
3. **Monopoly** — are you starting with a *big share of a small market*? (see [start small](start-small-and-monopolize.md))
4. **People** — do you have the *right team*?
5. **Distribution** — can you *deliver and sell*, not just build? (see [distribution](distribution-and-sales.md))
6. **Durability** — will your position be *defensible 10–20 years* out?
7. **Secret** — have you found a *unique opportunity others don't see*? (see [secrets](secrets-and-the-contrarian-question.md))

Thiel: nail all seven and you'll master fortune; miss several and you'll likely fail. (He uses Tesla as a company that, at the time, plausibly answered all seven; the 2000s clean-tech bust as firms that answered one or two.)

## How to apply

- **Score your idea 1–7 honestly.** A weak answer on **Distribution, Durability, or Monopoly** is a common silent killer that founders gloss over while obsessing over Engineering.
- Use it as a **pre-mortem** before committing years.
