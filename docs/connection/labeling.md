# Labeling ("It seems like…")

> **TL;DR.** Name the other person's emotion out loud — "It seems like you're frustrated," "It sounds
> like this really matters to you." Putting their feeling into words makes them feel understood, defuses
> negative emotion, and amplifies positive emotion. One of the highest-leverage rapport moves there is.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

A core skill from Chris Voss's *Never Split the Difference*: instead of arguing with or fixing someone's
emotion, you **acknowledge it by naming it**. The phrasings are deliberately tentative and *about them*,
not you:

- "It **seems like**…"
- "It **sounds like**…"
- "It **looks like**…"

Crucially, **avoid "I"** ("I hear that you…" makes it about you). The tentative opener also lets them
correct you, which keeps them talking. Labeling works because naming an emotion takes the charge out of
it — the same mechanism as [affect labeling](../eq/affect-labeling.md) for your *own* feelings, turned
on someone else's.

## How to apply

- When someone's emotional, **label before you respond**: "It seems like this has been really stressful."
  Then go quiet and let them react.
- Label the **negative** to defuse it ("It seems like you're worried I'll let you down") and the
  **positive** to amplify it ("It sounds like you're genuinely excited about this").
- A label they gently correct ("no, it's more that…") gets them explaining themselves — which deepens the
  exchange. Being slightly off is fine.
