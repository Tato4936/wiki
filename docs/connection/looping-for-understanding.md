# Looping for understanding

> **TL;DR.** A three-step move that *proves* you're listening: ask a real question, **summarize back** what
> you heard in your own words, then ask **"did I get that right?"** The summarize-and-check step is what
> turns ordinary listening into the kind people can feel.

## What it is

From Duhigg's *Supercommunicators* (building on decades of active-listening work), the **loop**:

1. **Ask** a question — ideally about how they feel or what matters, not just facts.
2. **Repeat back** what you heard, *in your own words*: "So it sounds like the part that stung wasn't the
   workload — it was being left out of the decision."
3. **Check**: "Did I get that right?" — and let them correct you.

Step 2 is the magic: a genuine paraphrase is **proof** you were listening (you can't fake it), and step 3
hands them control and invites them to go deeper. It's the most reliable way to make someone feel
[understood](perceived-partner-responsiveness.md).

## How to apply

- When something matters to the other person, **loop before you respond** — paraphrase the *feeling and the
  point*, then check. Hold your own take until they confirm you got theirs.
- Don't parrot their exact words — that's hollow. Put it in *your* words; the translation is what proves you
  understood.
