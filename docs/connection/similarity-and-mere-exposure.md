# Similarity & mere exposure

> **TL;DR.** Two of the most reliable, least glamorous drivers of liking: we like people who are
> **similar** to us, and we like people simply because they're **familiar**. Showing up repeatedly and
> finding genuine common ground does quiet, heavy lifting.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Two robust findings from social psychology:

- **Similarity-attraction** (Byrne): we're drawn to people who share our attitudes, values, background, and
  even mannerisms. Perceived similarity predicts liking strongly and consistently.
- **Mere exposure** (Zajonc): repeated exposure to someone increases liking, even with no interaction —
  familiarity itself breeds comfort. Proximity compounds it (Moreland & Beach found students liked
  classmates more the more often they'd simply *been seen* in the room, without ever speaking).

Neither is about charisma. They're why **repeated, low-key contact + authentic common ground** turns
strangers into friends — and why one-off dazzling impressions fade.

## How to apply

- **Show up consistently.** Recurring, low-stakes contact (the same gym, standup, coffee spot) builds
  liking through familiarity before you've done anything clever. Frequency beats intensity.
- **Find and surface genuine similarity** — shared experiences, tastes, values, humor. Don't fake it; do
  look for it and name it when it's real.
- For a relationship that matters, **engineer more touchpoints** rather than one grand gesture.
