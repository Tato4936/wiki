# Self-expansion

> **TL;DR.** People are drawn to relationships that *expand* them — that add new experiences, skills,
> perspectives, and a bigger sense of self. Doing novel, exciting things *together* bonds people more than
> pleasant-but-familiar routines. Be a source of expansion.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Arthur and Elaine Aron's **self-expansion model**: a core human motive is to grow the self — to gain new
perspectives, capabilities, and experiences — and we're attracted to people and relationships that help us
do it. Early attraction is partly the rush of rapid self-expansion: a new person opens whole new worlds.

The most-cited application: couples who do **novel and arousing activities together** report higher
relationship quality than those who do pleasant-but-familiar ones (Aron et al.). Shared *novelty* — not just
shared *time* — keeps a bond feeling alive; routine breeds boredom, which the model predicts.

## How to apply

- To deepen a relationship, **do new things together**, not just the same coffee — novelty and a little
  excitement get attributed to the *relationship* itself.
- Be **expanding to be around**: bring people new experiences, ideas, and worlds. Being a source of growth
  is magnetic in a way that merely being agreeable isn't.

## Sources

1. Aron, A., Norman, C. C., Aron, E. N., McKenna, C., & Heyman, R. E. (2000). Couples' shared participation in novel and arousing activities and experienced relationship quality. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 78, 273–284.
