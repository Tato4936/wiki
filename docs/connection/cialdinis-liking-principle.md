# Cialdini's liking principle

> **TL;DR.** We say yes to people we like — and Cialdini catalogued *what* drives liking: similarity,
> praise, cooperation toward shared goals, familiarity, and attractiveness. It's the persuasion-side map
> of everything else in this section.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

In *Influence*, Robert Cialdini lists **liking** as one of the core principles of persuasion: people are
far more willing to agree with, help, and trust those they like. He breaks down the main factors that
*produce* liking:

- **Similarity** — we like those like us (background, interests, style).
- **Praise / compliments** — we like those who like and appreciate us, even when we suspect a motive.
- **Cooperation** — working *together* toward a common goal (vs. competing) builds liking fast.
- **Familiarity** — repeated positive contact (see [mere exposure](similarity-and-mere-exposure.md)).
- **Physical attractiveness** — the "halo effect": we assume attractive people are also kind, smart, honest.

It's a handy index: most concrete rapport techniques are just ways of triggering one of these.

## How to apply

- To build rapport fast, **lean on similarity and cooperation**: find common ground, and frame things as
  "us vs. the problem," not "me vs. you."
- Give **genuine, specific** praise (see [specific compliments](specific-compliments.md)) — liking is
  reciprocal.
- Watch the flip side: knowing these factors helps you **notice when they're being used on you** — the
  too-friendly salesperson who "discovers" how much you have in common.
