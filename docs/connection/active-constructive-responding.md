# Active-constructive responding

> **TL;DR.** How you react to someone's **good** news matters more for the relationship than how you react
> to their bad news. Meet wins with genuine, engaged enthusiasm — get them to relive it — and the bond
> strengthens. Lukewarm or buzzkill responses quietly corrode it.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Shelly Gable's research on **capitalization** found that how partners respond when one shares something
*good* predicts relationship satisfaction and closeness — often more than support during the bad times.
There are four responses; only one builds connection:

- **Active-constructive** ✅ — enthusiastic, engaged, curious; help them savor it: "That's amazing — how
  did you find out? Tell me everything."
- **Passive-constructive** — quiet, understated: "Nice, good for you." (Underwhelming.)
- **Active-destructive** — find the downside: "Won't that mean longer hours?"
- **Passive-destructive** — ignore or hijack: "Oh, guess what *I* did today."

Most people *think* they're supportive but default to passive-constructive — pleasant but flat. The win is
to **visibly, curiously celebrate**.

## How to apply

- When someone shares good news, **stop what you're doing and get curious**: ask how it happened, let them
  re-tell it, match their excitement. Reliving it together is the whole point.
- Catch yourself before the reflexive "nice" or the instinctive "but…". Save any problem-spotting for much
  later.
