# Specific compliments (and just giving more)

> **TL;DR.** Two things about praise: make it **specific** (vague "great job" evaporates; "the way you
> reframed that objection was sharp" lands), and **give far more of it than you do** — people
> systematically undervalue how good their compliments make others feel, so they withhold them.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Two complementary points:

- **Specificity signals genuine attention.** A specific compliment proves you actually noticed *this*
  person and *this* thing, which is why it reads as sincere — and why generic praise feels like filler.
  (Carnegie's "honest and specific appreciation.")
- **We give too few.** Boothby & Bohns found people consistently **underestimate how positive a compliment
  makes the recipient feel**, and overestimate how awkward or bothered they'll be. So we silently sit on
  nice thoughts we have about people — a near-free way to make someone's day, left unspent.

## How to apply

- When you notice something good about someone, **say it — out loud, now, and specifically.** Name the
  exact thing: "you handled that interruption really gracefully," not "you're great."
- Distrust the "they'll think it's weird" reflex — the research says you're **underestimating the upside
  and overestimating the awkwardness.**
- Keep it **genuine**; a compliment you don't mean is flattery, and people can tell.
