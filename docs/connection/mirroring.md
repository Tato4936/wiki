# Mirroring

> **TL;DR.** Repeat the last one-to-three words the other person said, as a gentle question. It quietly
> says "tell me more," keeps them talking, and builds rapport — without you having to think of anything
> clever to say.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Another move from Voss's *Never Split the Difference*: a **"mirror"** is just echoing the **last few
words** (or the most important few) of what someone said, with a slight upward, curious inflection.

> — "We've been completely slammed this quarter."
> — "Completely slammed?"
> — "Yeah, the launch ate everyone's time, and honestly…"

It works because people feel compelled to fill the small silence and elaborate. It signals attention and
similarity (we trust people who seem like us), buys you a moment to think, and surfaces information — all
while you say almost nothing. Always pair it with a pause.

## How to apply

- When you want someone to **keep going** or **go deeper**, mirror their last few words and then **stay
  silent**. The silence does the work.
- Use it when you don't know what to ask — it's the lowest-effort way to keep a conversation alive and
  make the other person feel interesting.
- Don't overdo it (it gets parroty); a couple per conversation, on the emotionally loaded lines.
