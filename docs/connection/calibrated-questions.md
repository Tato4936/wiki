# Calibrated questions ("How…?" "What…?")

> **TL;DR.** Open-ended "How" and "What" questions hand the other person a feeling of control while
> getting them to solve your problem for you — "How am I supposed to do that?" beats a flat "No." They
> lower defensiveness and invite collaboration instead of a standoff.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Voss's **calibrated questions** are open questions — almost always starting with **"How"** or **"What"** —
that can't be answered yes/no and gently put the work on the other side:

- "**What** about this is important to you?"
- "**How** can we solve this together?"
- "**How** am I supposed to do that?" (a soft "no" that makes them reconsider)
- "**What** are we really trying to accomplish here?"

Two rules: **avoid "Why"** (it feels accusatory), and ask with genuine curiosity, not a cornering tone.
The questions make people feel autonomous and understood — which is what makes them cooperate, the
opposite of telling them what to do.

## How to apply

- When you'd normally **push back or refuse**, ask instead: "How am I supposed to do that?" — it makes
  *them* face the constraint, without conflict.
- To learn what someone actually wants, ask "**What's the biggest challenge** you're facing?" and listen
  for the need under the request.
- Swap accusatory "Why did you…?" for "**What led to…?**"
