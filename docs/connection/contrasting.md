# Contrasting

> **TL;DR.** When someone's misreading your intent ("so you think I'm useless?"), fix it with a
> **don't/do** statement: clarify what you *don't* mean, then what you *do* mean. "I don't think you're
> useless — I do think this plan needs another look." It de-escalates fast by restoring safety.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

From *Crucial Conversations*, **contrasting** is a repair move for when the other person feels attacked or
misjudges what you're saying. Two parts:

- **The don't:** address their fear / the misread — "I *don't* want you to think I don't value your work."
- **The do:** state your real meaning — "I *do* want us to hit the deadline without burning out."

It works because most defensiveness comes from a **mistaken story** about your intent. You're not
apologizing or backing off the substance — you're removing the *misunderstanding* so the real conversation
can continue. Use it the instant you see someone bristle.

## How to apply

- When someone reacts as if you said something worse than you meant, **contrast immediately**: "That's not
  what I'm saying — here's what I am." Don't argue the substance until the misread is cleared.
- Use it *preemptively* before a sensitive point too: "I don't want this to sound like criticism; I do want
  to share something I noticed."
