# Bids for connection

> **TL;DR.** People constantly make tiny "bids" for attention — a comment, a question, a look. Whether you
> **turn toward** them (engage), turn away (ignore), or turn against (rebuff) is, over time, what makes or
> breaks closeness. Catching and answering small bids is the quiet engine of strong relationships.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

From Gottman's research, a **bid** is any small attempt to connect: "Look at that bird," "Ugh, long day,"
forwarding a meme, a sigh that invites a "what's up?" Each bid gets one of three responses:

- **Turning toward** ✅ — engage, even briefly: "Oh yeah? Where?"
- **Turning away** — miss it or ignore it (often not malicious, just distracted).
- **Turning against** — snap or dismiss: "Can't you see I'm busy?"

Gottman found couples who stayed together turned toward bids the large majority of the time, while those
who split turned away far more. It scales to *every* relationship: connection is built in hundreds of
these micro-moments, not in grand gestures.

## How to apply

- **Notice bids and turn toward them** — especially the small, easy-to-miss ones. A three-second engaged
  response deposits more than you'd think.
- Too busy to fully engage? **Acknowledge the bid anyway** ("I want to hear about this — give me ten
  minutes?"). A turned-away bid stings more than people realize.
- Make bids yourself, and notice who turns toward *yours* — it's a quiet signal of who's invested.
