# Visible effort

> **TL;DR.** Effort is a costly, hard-to-fake signal of how much you value someone — but only the
> effort they can *perceive* counts. Make the care legible; never advertise the cost.

## What it is

Two mechanisms stack:

- **Effort is a costly signal.** Talk is cheap; effort isn't. Because spending time, attention, and
  thought on someone genuinely costs you, it's a *credible* signal that you value them — one words
  can't fake. (The logic of Zahavi's costly-signal / handicap principle, carried over to relationships.)
- **The effort heuristic.** People use *perceived* effort as a shortcut for quality and worth: the same
  gift, gesture, or piece of work is judged better and more meaningful when it looks like more effort
  went in (Kruger et al., 2004). Perceived effort doesn't just read as sincere — it inflates how much
  the gesture is valued.

The corollary does the real work: **invisible effort signals nothing.** Effort you spent but they never
perceive buys you nothing in their eyes — and quietly breeds your own resentment. The signal lives in
what's *legible to them*, not in what you actually spent.

**The one caveat that flips it:** show the *care*, not the *cost*. Effort lands when it's visibly **for
them** (you remembered, you tailored it, you showed up). It backfires the moment you draw attention to
what it cost **you** — announcing your sacrifice reads as fishing for credit, and humblebragging about
it lands worse than saying nothing (Sezer et al., 2018).

## How to apply

- **Make at least one strand of the effort perceptible.** Reference the specific thing ("I went by that
  bookshop you mentioned"), don't just silently hand over the result.
- **Tailor over generic.** A small, specifically-fitted gesture out-signals a large generic one, because
  specificity *is* the visible proof of attention. (See [specific compliments](specific-compliments.md).)
- **Go first.** Paying a small cost to open trust is itself a legible-effort move — the engine under the
  [vulnerability loop](vulnerability-loop.md).
- **Never invoice it.** No "do you know how long that took me?" The instant effort is presented as a
  debt, it stops reading as care.
- **Audit your own ledger.** If you're spending effort no one can see and resenting it, the fix is
  usually to *redirect* it to something perceptible — not to spend more.
