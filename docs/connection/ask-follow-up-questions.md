# Ask follow-up questions

> **TL;DR.** The simplest, best-tested way to be liked in conversation: ask more questions — especially
> **follow-up** questions that build on what they just said. It proves you're listening and that you care,
> and people consistently like question-askers more.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Across experiments — including real speed-dates — Huang and colleagues found that people who **ask more
questions are better liked**, and that the effect is driven by **follow-up questions**: questions that
respond to and extend what the other person just said. Follow-ups work because they're hard evidence of
listening and responsiveness; they say "I heard you, and I want more."

Most people under-ask, because they're rehearsing what *they'll* say next, or they worry questions are
intrusive. The data says the opposite: asking helps you, and costs almost nothing.

## How to apply

- **Default to a follow-up.** After someone speaks, your first instinct should be a question that builds on
  it — not a comment about yourself. "What was that like?" "How'd you end up there?"
- Aim for **genuine curiosity**, not interrogation — follow the thread that actually interests you.
- This is the on-ramp to everything else here: [mirroring](mirroring.md), [labeling](labeling.md), and
  [calibrated questions](calibrated-questions.md) are all just structured ways to ask.
