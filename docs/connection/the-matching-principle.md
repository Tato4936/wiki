# The matching principle

> **TL;DR.** Every conversation is really one of three kinds — **practical** (let's solve/decide),
> **emotional** (I want to feel heard), or **social** (who we are to each other). Most miscommunication is
> two people in *different* kinds. Figure out which is happening and **match it** before you respond.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Charles Duhigg's synthesis in *Supercommunicators*: beneath the topic, a conversation is driven at any
moment by one of three mindsets —

- **Practical** ("What's this *really* about?") — making a decision, solving a problem.
- **Emotional** ("How do we *feel*?") — wanting empathy, not solutions.
- **Social** ("Who are *we*?") — identity, relationships, belonging.

The classic failure: someone shares a problem **emotionally** ("I'm so done with my boss") and you answer
**practically** ("here's what you should do…"). You're technically helpful and totally mismatched — they
wanted to feel heard, not fixed. Connection requires being in the **same kind of conversation** first.

## How to apply

- Before responding, ask: **is this person practical, emotional, or social right now?** When unsure, a quick
  "do you want help thinking it through, or do you just need to vent?" matches you on purpose.
- The most common miss is answering an **emotional** bid with a **practical** fix — the
  [fixing reflex](../eq/active-listening-rogers.md). Match the emotion first; solutions land later, if at all.
