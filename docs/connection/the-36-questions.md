# The 36 questions (escalating disclosure)

> **TL;DR.** Closeness isn't only found, it can be *built*: take turns answering questions that get
> gradually more personal, and two strangers feel remarkably close in under an hour. The active
> ingredient is **sustained, escalating, mutual self-disclosure** — not the specific questions.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Arthur Aron's experiment paired strangers who worked through **36 questions** escalating from light
("Would you like to be famous? In what way?") to deeply personal ("When did you last cry in front of
another person?"), each answering in turn — capped with four minutes of eye contact. Pairs reported
closeness comparable to long-standing relationships.

The mechanism is general: **reciprocal, gradually deepening vulnerability builds intimacy.** One person
opens up a little, the other matches it, and the escalation does the work. The questions are just
scaffolding for that loop.

## How to apply

- To deepen any relationship, **trade disclosures and let them escalate** — share something a notch more
  personal than small talk, and if they match it, keep climbing. Reciprocity is the rule; don't dump.
- Replace interview-mode "what do you do?" with questions that invite a *real* answer ("what's something
  you've changed your mind about lately?").
- It works on existing relationships too — couples and friends who keep disclosing stay close.
