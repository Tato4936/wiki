# Support vs. shift response

> **TL;DR.** When someone shares something, you can give a **support response** (keep the spotlight on them
> — "what happened next?") or a **shift response** (turn it to yourself — "oh, that reminds me of when
> I…"). Habitually shifting is "conversational narcissism," and it quietly starves connection.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Sociologist Charles Derber's distinction (*The Pursuit of Attention*). Someone says, "We just got back from
Italy — it was exhausting." Two replies:

- **Shift response:** "Oh, I *love* Italy, when I went we…" → spotlight now on *you*.
- **Support response:** "Exhausting how? What did you pack into it?" → spotlight stays on *them*.

A little sharing is fine — it builds [similarity](similarity-and-mere-exposure.md). But people who
*reflexively* shift — turning every topic back to their own experience — practice what Derber calls
**conversational narcissism**, and it leaves others feeling unheard even though nothing rude was said.

## How to apply

- Notice your default. After someone shares, does your reply keep the spotlight **on them** or pull it **to
  you**? Support first; share *after* they feel heard.
- "That reminds me of my…" is the tell. Before you use it, give at least one genuine
  [support response / follow-up](ask-follow-up-questions.md).
