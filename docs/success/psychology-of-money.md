# The psychology of money

> **TL;DR.** Morgan Housel's counterweight to "get rich" hustle: doing well with money is **behavioral, not technical.** The load-bearing ideas — **survival first** (avoid ruin so compounding can run), **tails drive everything**, **"enough"** (knowing when to stop), and **room for error** — are about *staying* wealthy, a different skill from *getting* wealthy.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-06

## What it is

The contrast with the rest of this section: Altman and Naval are about *getting* rich; Housel is about *keeping* it — and staying sane.

- **Getting vs. staying wealthy.** Getting wealthy takes optimism and risk; *staying* wealthy takes the opposite — frugality, paranoia, **survival.** Compounding only works if you're never knocked out, so *avoiding ruin* beats maximizing returns.
- **Tails drive everything.** A tiny fraction of events and decisions produce most outcomes (the [power law](../startups/the-power-law.md) again) — so being wrong often is fine if you're huge on the few that matter.
- **"Enough."** The hardest financial skill is knowing when you have enough and stopping the goalposts from moving — *"there is no reason to risk what you have and need for what you don't have and don't need."*
- **Room for error.** Plan to survive being wrong; endurance is the most important variable.

## How to apply

- **Make avoiding ruin the first rule** — leverage, over-concentration, and lifestyle creep that could knock you out cost more than the upside they add.
- **Define "enough" in advance**, before the goalposts move.
- **Build slack** (cash, low fixed costs) so a bad year doesn't end the game.
