# Specific knowledge

> **TL;DR.** Naval's idea: build **specific knowledge** — expertise you *can't be trained for*, found by following genuine obsession. *"If you can be trained for it, so can someone else"* — then you compete on price. Specific knowledge is the un-automatable, un-commoditizable skill that makes you the only person who can do your particular thing.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-06

## What it is

*"Specific knowledge is found by pursuing your genuine curiosity… rather than whatever is hot right now."* Its signatures: it **can't be taught in a classroom** (no course, no certificate), it sits at the **bleeding edge** of a field, and — the tell — *building it "will feel like play to you but look like work to others."*

Why it matters: anything trainable gets **commoditized** (someone cheaper, or a model, learns it too). Specific knowledge is your defensible edge — the human, idiosyncratic, often technical-or-creative skill that resists outsourcing and automation. *"Read what you love until you love to read"* is the on-ramp: follow real curiosity until you've gone deeper than anyone told you to.

## How to apply

- **Follow genuine obsession**, not the hot field — the thing you'd learn anyway is where you out-compound everyone chasing the trend.
- **Watch for what feels like play to you but work to others** — that gap is your specific knowledge forming.
- Pair it with [leverage](leverage.md) and [ownership](own-things-not-time.md): rare skill × scale × equity = wealth.
