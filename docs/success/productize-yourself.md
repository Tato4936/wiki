# Productize yourself

> **TL;DR.** Two moves from Naval (via Scott Adams): **(1)** combine **2–3 above-average skills** into a rare *intersection* competitors can't copy — *"you can't be normal and expect abnormal returns"*; **(2)** learn to **build *and* sell.** Then **productize** the result — turn the skill-stack into something that scales (a product, brand, body of work) instead of a service billed by the hour.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-06

## What it is

You don't need to be the *best* at one thing (a brutal, narrow race). The **talent stack**: be top-~25% at two or three complementary skills — *engineering × writing × design*, or *quant × communication × a domain* — and their **intersection** is nearly unique. This is also Altman's *"be hard to compete with"* (#10): build value *"at the intersection of two or three areas."*

Then **learn to sell, not just build.** Naval: *"I'd rather teach an engineer marketing than a marketer engineering"* — building is the slow foundation; selling can be layered on top, and you need both. Altman's #4 ("get good at sales") agrees, with the key caveat: *"the best way to be good at sales is to genuinely believe in what you're selling."*

**Productize** = give the stack [leverage](leverage.md): a product, software, or media output that earns without your continuous time.

## How to apply

- **Pick your intersection:** which 2–3 skills do you have that rarely co-occur? Deepen the *stack* rather than chasing #1 in one lane.
- **Add selling** to your building — persuasion, writing, distribution (see [distribution & sales](../startups/distribution-and-sales.md)).
- **Package it** into something replicable, not a bespoke hourly service.
