# Own things, don't rent your time

> **TL;DR.** *"You get rich by owning things"* (Altman) — equity, assets, IP that earn while you sleep — **not** by selling hours for a salary. Altman: *"almost no one in the history of the Forbes list has gotten there with a salary."* A wage is capped and stops when you stop; ownership compounds and detaches income from your time.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-06

## What it is

Naval's distinction: **wealth** = *"assets that earn while you sleep"* (businesses, investments, IP); **money** = *"IOUs from society"* for past value; **status** = a zero-sum social rank. Chase wealth, not money or status.

The mechanism: a salary is **renting out your time** — linear, capped, and it ends the moment you stop. Ownership (equity, royalties, a business, invested capital) keeps earning and **compounds**. Naval's deliberately provocative line: *"the most dangerous things are heroin and a monthly salary"* — both quietly trap you. The aim is enough owned, compounding assets that your income no longer depends on your hours.

## How to apply

- **Take equity over cash** where you can afford the risk — own a piece of what you build, don't just bill for it.
- **Convert earnings into owned, compounding assets** (a business, index funds, IP) rather than lifestyle.
- For the long game, route ownership through [wealth structuring](../wealth-structuring/index.md): hold appreciating assets, borrow against them, defer tax.
