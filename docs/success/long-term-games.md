# Long-term games with long-term people

> **TL;DR.** Naval: *"play long-term games with long-term people."* Returns in wealth, knowledge, and relationships all come from **compound interest** — and compounding only runs when you keep iterating with the *same* trustworthy people. Reputation and trust are the compounding assets; betrayal (theirs or yours) resets the curve to zero.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-06

## What it is

Iterated games change behavior: when you'll deal with someone *repeatedly* for decades, cooperation and integrity become rational, not just nice. *"All the returns in life come from compound interest"* — so you want long games (time for compounding) with long-term partners (so the relationship itself compounds).

Naval's partner filter: **intelligence, energy, and integrity** — *"and if they don't have the third, the first two will kill you."* Also: *"don't partner with cynics and pessimists — their beliefs are self-fulfilling."* Industry matters too: where **reputation persists** (tech, investing) the long game pays; in rotating-door fields it doesn't.

## How to apply

- **Pick a few long-term people** (partners, collaborators, friends) and invest in them for decades, instead of transacting widely and shallowly.
- **Optimize for trust and reputation** — the assets that compound and open every future door.
- **Screen for integrity first** in anyone whose outcomes you'd tie to yours.
