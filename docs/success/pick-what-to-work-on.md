# Pick what to work on

> **TL;DR.** Paul Graham's recipe (*How to Do Great Work*): aim for the intersection of **natural aptitude**, **deep interest**, and **scope to do great work.** Because of the [power law](../startups/the-power-law.md), *which* problem you choose dominates almost everything you do afterward — so the choice deserves far more thought than people give it.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-06

## What it is

Graham: do great work by *"working on something you have a natural aptitude for, a deep interest in, and that offers scope to do great work."* The engine underneath is **genuine curiosity** — find something *"so interesting to you that you'd do it even if you didn't have to."* (Naval's [specific knowledge](specific-knowledge.md) and Altman's "compound yourself" point the same way.)

Two warnings:

- **Don't follow fashion.** Chasing what's prestigious or hot lands you competing with everyone and discovering nothing.
- **The meta-decision dominates.** Outcomes are power-law, so picking the right *thing* is worth more than years of execution on the wrong one — Altman's "focus on the right thing" (#6) and the [power law](../startups/the-power-law.md) agree.

## How to apply

- **Audit the three circles:** what are you unusually good at × genuinely curious about × where great work is still possible? Hunt the overlap.
- **Follow curiosity over prestige** — the obsession you'd pursue anyway is where you'll out-work everyone.
- **Spend real time on the choice itself** — it's the highest-leverage decision you'll make.
