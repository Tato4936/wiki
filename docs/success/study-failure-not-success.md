# Study failure, not success (Justin Sun)

> **TL;DR.** Success stories are nearly useless to copy — overdetermined, luck-laced, survivorship-biased. **Failure is the teachable part.** Sun: "people in business want even more to study how to fail — avoiding those failures is itself a necessary condition for success." Invert: catalogue how others died, and don't do that.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-08

*Distilled second-hand from a community-compiled "Justin Sun thinking" file; quotes attributed to his autobiography and interviews, not independently verified. Sun is a polarizing figure — see [Attention as value](../startups/attention-as-value.md).*

## What it is

Outsiders study how winners won; operators study how losers lost — because winning is overdetermined and not replicable, while **failure modes recur and are avoidable.** Sun (crediting Jack Ma's Hupan University) gives cases: LeEco ran *seven* ecosystems at once and so fought "1-against-5" everywhere; and his own early ICO loss (tuition paid). 

This is **inversion / via negativa** — the same engine as Munger's "invert, always invert" and "avoid stupidity rather than seek brilliance," and Taleb's via negativa — here in Sun's articulation. The practical form is the **pre-mortem**: assume the venture failed, and ask *why*.

## How to apply

- **List the graveyard first:** who tried this and died, and of what? Run a pre-mortem before committing.
- **Optimize for not-losing** (avoid ruin and known failure modes) *before* optimizing for winning.
- **Distrust success-story playbooks** (survivorship); trust the failure patterns.
