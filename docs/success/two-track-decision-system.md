# The two-track decision system (CZ)

> **TL;DR.** Match decision *speed* to *reversibility* and *stakes*. For escalated 50/50 calls, don't agonize — effectively flip a coin, move, and **course-correct fast** ("20–40% of my decisions are not right, but you find out quickly and correct quickly"). Reserve slow deliberation for the genuinely **irreversible, high-stakes** ones. Indecision is the real failure.

## What it is

Changpeng Zhao (CZ, Binance founder) runs two modes:

- **Fast track.** When a dispute is escalated to you, it's ~50/50 by definition — "if it were a clear decision they would have agreed already." So pick one and move; fixing a wrong call quickly beats stalling. "It is usually much better to make a decision then execute than not making a decision."
- **Structured track** (from CZ's hand-written *Principles*), for weighty calls, in order: (1) **Core principle?** — if it touches fairness / users / ethics, follow the principle, full stop; (2) **small vs. big** — small → decide fast or delegate; big → gather data, small-group discuss, sleep on it; (3) **reversible vs. permanent** — try reversible things boldly, treat irreversible ones (large M&A, big bets) with caution; (4) **do I have expertise?** — decide fast where you do, delegate where you don't; (5) **sufficient info** — don't wait for certainty.

The unifying move: classify *before* deciding (reversible? small? in my expertise?), and let that set the speed.

## How to apply

- **Label the decision first.** Reversible, small, or in your wheelhouse → decide now.
- **Spend deliberation only on one-way doors** (irreversible, high-stakes).
- **For genuine 50/50 ties, pick and move** — build a habit of fast correction over agonizing.
