# Increment over stock (Justin Sun)

> **TL;DR.** Don't anchor on what you *have* — savings, a degree, a current valuation (the **stock**); anchor on what you can *create next* — growth rate, slope, trajectory (the **increment**). A large stock growing at zero is a dead position; a small base compounding fast is a live one. Markets price the slope, not the level.

## What it is

Justin Sun (TRON founder) treats **growth rate** as the master variable. Stock thinking counts degrees, real estate, cash on hand; increment thinking counts trajectory and future potential. He reads companies as **"mountain-climbing camps"** — each order of magnitude (10M → 100M → 1B → 10B…) a camp, and the only question that matters is "can it sprint to the *next* camp?", not "how big is it now?" It rhymes with how markets reward high-growth over high-but-flat earnings.

The trap it warns against: **defending a large stock** — accumulated status, prestige, a sunk position — at the cost of growth. A prestigious-but-stagnant path can lose to a humble-but-compounding one.

## How to apply

- **Judge yourself and projects by slope:** what's the growth rate, and where's the ceiling?
- **Don't over-protect status/sunk prestige** when it caps your growth rate.
- **Pick the steepest *sustainable* compounding curve** (pairs with [compound yourself](compound-yourself.md)).
