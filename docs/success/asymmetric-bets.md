# Asymmetric bets

> **TL;DR.** Altman's #5: **make it easy to take risks**, especially young. People *overestimate* risk and *underestimate* reward. Look for **asymmetric** bets — *"you lose 1× if you're wrong but make 100× if it's right."* Because outcomes follow a [power law](../startups/the-power-law.md), a few of those wins dominate a whole career — so take *more* cheap shots at them, not fewer.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-06

## What it is

*"It's easier to take risks when you have less to lose"* — few obligations, low burn, time to recover — so a failed bet costs little when you're young, while the upside is uncapped. People systematically misjudge this: the downside of "it didn't work" is usually a survivable setback; the upside of "it worked" can be life-changing.

This is **convexity**: bounded loss, unbounded gain. Combined with the [power law](../startups/the-power-law.md) (a handful of bets produce most lifetime returns), the rational move is to **maximize exposure to asymmetric upside** while you can afford it — many small, cheap, high-ceiling bets — rather than defending a comfortable downside.

!!! note "Asymmetric ≠ reckless"
    The point is *bounded* downside with *large* upside — not betting the farm. Ruinous, un-survivable risks break compounding (see [psychology of money](psychology-of-money.md)); keep the losses 1×, never −∞.

## How to apply

- **Engineer low fixed costs** while young (low burn, optionality) so you can *afford* to take shots.
- **Prefer capped-loss, uncapped-upside bets** — startups, building in public, cold outreach, new skills.
- **Take more of them** — under a power law, more cheap tickets *with real edge* beats one safe path.
