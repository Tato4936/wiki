# Habit stacking & temptation bundling

> **TL;DR.** Two cue tricks. **Habit stacking:** anchor a new habit to one you already do — "*After* [existing habit], I will [new habit]" — borrowing an existing, reliable cue. **Temptation bundling:** only let yourself have a *want* while doing a *should* — audiobook only at the gym — so the craving pulls you toward the chore.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium–high · **Updated:** 2026-06-08

## What it is

**Habit stacking** (Clear's term; rooted in BJ Fogg's *Tiny Habits* "anchoring") solves the hardest part of a new habit — remembering to do it — by chaining it to an established routine. The old habit *is* the cue: "After I pour my morning coffee, I will write my three priorities." It's [implementation intentions](implementation-intentions.md) with the cue chosen for maximum reliability (you already do the anchor every day).

**Temptation bundling** (Milkman, Minson & Volpp, 2014) couples an instantly-gratifying activity with a beneficial-but-tedious one. In their field experiment, letting people listen to page-turner audiobooks *only* at the gym raised gym attendance — the immediate reward solves the habit loop's weakest link (beneficial habits usually have *delayed* payoffs, so they don't reinforce). Bundling supplies an immediate reward on the spot.

⚠️ Both are practitioner-grade tactics (temptation bundling has one strong field study; the effect faded somewhat over time). Treat as useful defaults, not laws.

## How to apply

- **Stack onto rock-solid anchors:** existing habits tied to time/place — after brushing teeth, after sitting at your desk, after the first coffee.
- **Keep the new habit tiny at first** so the stack never breaks (→ [how habits form](how-habits-form.md)).
- **Bundle a guilty pleasure with a should:** favorite podcast only while doing chores; specialty coffee only while reviewing finances.
