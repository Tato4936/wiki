# Implementation intentions

> **TL;DR.** The single best-evidenced way to close the gap between intending and doing: pre-decide the **when and where** as an if–then plan — **"When situation X arises, I will do Y."** Specifying the cue in advance hands control to the situation, so the behavior fires automatically instead of waiting on in-the-moment motivation.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-08

## What it is

Goal intentions ("I want to exercise more") specify an outcome; **implementation intentions** (Gollwitzer, 1999) specify the *situated action*: "**If** it's 7am and I've had coffee, **then** I put on my running shoes." You decide once, ahead of time, and link a concrete cue to a concrete response.

The evidence is unusually strong for a behavior tactic: Gollwitzer & Sheeran's (2006) meta-analysis of **94 studies** found a **medium-to-large effect (d ≈ 0.65)** on goal attainment, *on top of* goal intentions alone. It works because the if-then plan pre-loads the cue: when X shows up, Y is triggered without re-deliberation — effectively manufacturing a [habit cue](the-habit-loop.md) on purpose. It's especially powerful for goals people *want* but keep failing to act on (the intention–action gap), and pairs well with "coping plans" for predictable obstacles ("If I'm tired after work, then I do just 10 minutes").

## How to apply

- **Write it literally as "When [specific cue], I will [specific action]."** Vague cues ("when I get a chance") don't work; concrete time/place/preceding-event cues do.
- **Anchor to something already stable** in your day (→ [habit stacking](habit-stacking-and-temptation-bundling.md)).
- **Pre-plan the obstacle:** add an if-then for the failure mode you can already predict.
- Use it to operationalize anything in your [focus](../focus/time-blocking.md) or training routine.
