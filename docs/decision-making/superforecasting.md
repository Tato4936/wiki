# Superforecasting

> **TL;DR.** Forecasting is a **trainable, measurable skill**, not a gift. In a multi-year tournament, ordinary "superforecasters" beat credentialed analysts — through *how* they think: base rates first, fine-grained probabilities, and many small updates. "**Foxes**" (many models, flexible) beat "**hedgehogs**" (one big idea).

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Philip Tetlock's earlier work showed pundits barely beat chance, and that **foxes** out-predict **hedgehogs**. The Good Judgment Project (an IARPA forecasting tournament) then found a top ~2% — "**superforecasters**" — who reliably beat professionals, scored objectively by **Brier score** (calibration against real outcomes).

Their habits are the point — and all learnable:

- Break a vague question into estimable parts ("**Fermi-ize**").
- Start from the **base rate** (outside view), *then* adjust for specifics (inside view).
- Think in **fine-grained probabilities** (73%, not "likely") and **update in small increments** as news arrives — the single strongest predictor of accuracy was **"perpetual beta,"** the willingness to keep revising.
- **Actively open-minded thinking:** treat your beliefs as hypotheses to test, not possessions to defend.

Even brief probabilistic-reasoning training measurably improved accuracy.

## How to apply

- **Put numbers on your predictions** and write them down — you can't improve calibration you don't track.
- **Anchor on the base rate first.** "How often do things like this turn out X?" beats reasoning from the vivid specifics.
- **Update often and a little.** Don't wait for certainty; nudge your probability as evidence trickles in.
