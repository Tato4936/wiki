# Noise vs. bias

> **TL;DR.** Bad judgments fail two ways. **Bias** is a systematic miss in one direction (everyone shades high). **Noise** is random *scatter* — two equally qualified people judging the same case reach very different answers. Organizations obsess over bias and ignore noise, even though noise is often just as costly — and it stays invisible because you usually see only one judgment per case.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

From Kahneman, Sibony & Sunstein (2021). The target analogy: **bias** = shots clustered off-center the same way; **noise** = shots sprayed all over. Both wreck accuracy.

The killer example: at an insurance company, the premiums **different underwriters** independently set for the **same** cases differed by a median of **55%** — about five times the ~10% executives expected. The same scatter turns up in judges' sentences, medical diagnoses, hiring, and asylum rulings.

Noise decomposes into **level noise** (some judges are just harsher overall), **pattern noise** (idiosyncratic reactions to particular cases), and **occasion noise** (the *same* judge differing by mood, fatigue, even the weather).

## How to apply

- **Aggregate independent judgments.** Average several people who *haven't* talked to each other — independence is what kills noise (a committee that anchors on the first speaker doesn't count).
- **Use rules, checklists, or simple formulas** for repeated decisions; they're noise-free, which is much of why they often beat expert gut.
- **"Decision hygiene":** break a judgment into independently-scored parts and delay the holistic gut-call to the end (Kahneman's Mediating Assessments Protocol). Like hand-washing — you prevent errors you can't see one at a time.
- For *your own* recurring calls (hiring, ratings, reviews), **fix a structure in advance** instead of judging each one holistically in the moment.
