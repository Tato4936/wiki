# Partner archetypes

!!! warning "Contested — stereotypes + pop evolutionary psychology"
    These are sweeping generalizations about *types* of men, several resting on pop evo-psych and
    pop-psychoanalysis ("paternity uncertainty," a "patricide complex"). Useful at most as loose
    hypotheses for noticing patterns; unreliable and unfair applied to an individual. Don't profile a
    real person off a cartoon.

> **TL;DR.** The source sorts high-value men into four archetypes — self-made founder, second-gen
> rich, steady professional, ordinary earner — and scores each on five axes (wealth, willingness-to-
> give, emotional value, loyalty, companionship). The five-axis lens is a decent checklist; the
> archetypes and their "psychologies" are caricatures.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** low · **Updated:** 2026-06-03

## The five-axis lens (the portable part)

Every archetype is rated on **wealth tier · willingness to give · emotional value · loyalty ·
companionship time.** Stripped of the agenda, this is a reasonable **compatibility checklist** — most
real tradeoffs (a high earner with no time; a present partner with less money) do live on these axes.

## The four archetypes (caricatured)

| Archetype | Sketch | The "catch" the source names |
|-----------|--------|------------------------------|
| **创一代 — self-made founder** | High but volatile wealth; treats love like work; wants a "life-partner," not just a pretty face | Little time / emotional value; control issues; "be there at his low point" |
| **富二代 — second-gen rich** | Inherited wealth; avoids commitment; "plays the field" | Marriage is decided by *his family* — win the parents, not just him |
| **好用上进男 — steady professional** | Programmer / doctor / civil servant; frugal, loyal; the "no short board" pick | Low emotional expressiveness; framed as **avoidant attachment** |
| **经济适用男 — ordinary earner** | Average income; competes on time + loyalty because he must | Limited money; "you'll build the household together" |

On top of this the source layers **pop evolutionary psychology** ("rich-man / playboy / nice-guy"
mating strategies) and **pop psychoanalysis** (the second-gen's "patricide complex," the founder's
"cuckoldry fear"). Treat all of that as **storytelling, not science.**

## The one genuinely useful thread: avoidant attachment

The "steady professional" sketch is really a folk description of **avoidant attachment** — someone
raised to suppress emotional expression, who needs space, dislikes conflict, and reads as "cold"
without being uncaring. That maps (loosely) onto a real construct from attachment theory, and the
practical takeaway is reasonable: *don't escalate conflict to force closeness from an avoidant
partner; build it through low-pressure consistency.* The source's version is oversimplified —
attachment style is a spectrum, not a man-type.

## How to apply

- Use the **five axes** as a compatibility checklist; treat the **archetypes** as nothing more than
  loose priors.
- Never diagnose a real person from a category — that's how you misread the individual in front of you.
- The [value-exchange model](the-value-exchange-model.md) underlies all of this; read its limits.
