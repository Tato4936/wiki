# Marriage: maintenance & financial power

!!! note "Mixed — some of this echoes real relationship science"
    Unusually, parts of this section converge with evidence-based advice (ask for needs without
    criticism; give a partner decompression time; lower fairy-tale expectations). Other parts are the
    familiar transactional / control frame (keep providing "emotional value" or he'll stray; hold the
    finances as security). Kept with that split marked.

> **TL;DR.** The marriage advice mixes genuinely sound communication habits — soft requests over
> criticism, managing expectations, not comparing your partner to others — with a transactional core
> (keep supplying "emotional value," and hold the household finances as your security).

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** low · **Updated:** 2026-06-03

## The parts that match the research

- **Ask for needs without criticism.** "I love it when you massage me" instead of "you never massage
  me." This is almost verbatim the [Gottman](../eq/gottman-ratio-and-four-horsemen.md) **soft-startup /
  avoid-criticism** finding — a real, well-supported move.
- **Don't compare him to other men.** Comparison breeds contempt — also straight out of the Gottman
  risk factors.
- **Give him decompression time** after work before piling on problems; **lower fairy-tale
  expectations** of marriage. Reasonable emotion-regulation and expectation management.
- **Don't rush to divorce over small recurring conflicts** — consistent with the research finding that
  most couple conflict is *perpetual*, not resolvable.

## The transactional core (read critically)

- **"Provide emotional value or he'll stray."** Frames care as a maintenance obligation against a
  cheating baseline — corrosive, and a direct instance of [the value-exchange model](the-value-exchange-model.md).
- **"Security = holding the household finances."** The source defines safety as controlling the money
  ("money in my hands, he behaves") and pitches it as healthy *mutual* leverage; in practice it slides
  toward control. The one portable idea underneath — **keep financial independence and literacy; don't
  be fully dependent** — is sound. The "seize the purse" framing is not.

## How to apply

- **Keep:** soft requests, no comparisons, decompression time, realistic expectations, your own
  financial footing. Most of that is just good, evidence-aligned relationship practice.
- **Drop:** the "earn his love by supplying value or lose him" anxiety, and the finances-as-leverage
  game.
