# Infidelity: the source's claims, fact-checked

!!! warning "Contested — shaky stats and pop evolutionary determinism"
    This section asserts very high male-infidelity rates and frames cheating as biologically
    inevitable. The academic figure has a real source but limited generalizability; the viral "61%"
    is an online poll, not evidence. The "men are wired to cheat" framing is **pop evolutionary
    psychology** — contested, and routinely used to excuse behavior. Some "prevention" advice tips
    into surveillance and intimidation. Captured critically.

> **TL;DR.** The source claims most men cheat (citing 34.8% and 61.2% figures), explains it via
> evo-psych, and advises setting boundaries early plus keeping novelty. Setting boundaries early is
> reasonable; the determinism and the intimidation tactics are not.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** low · **Updated:** 2026-06-03

## The claims, checked

- **"34.8% of married men have cheated"** — attributed to sexologist **Pan Suiming (潘绥铭)**, a real
  and respected researcher of Chinese sexual behavior. Plausibly real, but any single survey figure
  swings with sample, definition, and year; don't treat it as a fixed constant.
- **"61.2% of men in relationships have cheated"** — a 2019 Tencent online poll. **Self-selected
  internet polls are not reliable evidence.** Discount heavily.
- **"Men are biologically driven to cheat"** — leans on "males spread genes, females invest"
  storytelling. This is **contested pop evolutionary psychology**; even where there's signal,
  *natural ≠ inevitable ≠ excusable.* Treat as ideology.

## Reasonable vs not

- **Reasonable:** stating boundaries and expectations *early* rather than after a betrayal; not
  outsourcing your security entirely to a partner's character. (Echoes the one good point in
  [the value-exchange model](the-value-exchange-model.md).)
- **Not reasonable:** the confrontation script built on **bluffing omniscience** ("I always know —
  don't ask how") to intimidate, and the surveillance framing in general. That's a control tactic,
  not a relationship.

## How to apply

- Take one thing: relationships do better when expectations and boundaries are explicit and early.
  Everything else here — the determinism, the mind-games — flag and leave.
