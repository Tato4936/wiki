# Spaced repetition

> **TL;DR.** **Spread study out over time instead of cramming.** The same total hours produce far more durable memory when sessions are spaced across days/weeks than massed into one block. Review material roughly *as you're about to forget it*.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

The **spacing effect** is one of the oldest and most robust findings in memory research (back to Ebbinghaus). Cepeda et al.'s (2006) meta-analysis confirmed spaced practice reliably beats massed practice for long-term retention, and that the **optimal gap grows with how long you need to remember** — to retain something for months, space reviews over weeks.

Massing (cramming) inflates *short-term* performance, which is why it survives — it works for tomorrow's exam and fails the month after. Spacing is a [desirable difficulty](desirable-difficulties.md): each review is a little harder because you've partly forgotten, and that effortful re-retrieval is what cements it. Dunlosky rated **distributed practice among the two highest-utility techniques.**

## How to apply

- **Schedule reviews across days, not one marathon.** 4×1hr on separate days ≫ one 4hr block.
- **Use a spaced-repetition system (SRS)** like Anki/Leitner — it shows each card *just before* you'd forget, expanding intervals automatically.
- **Pair with [retrieval](retrieval-practice.md):** spaced *self-testing*, not spaced *rereading*.
- For exams, start early and revisit; "distributed cramming" still beats one all-nighter.
