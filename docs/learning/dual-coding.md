# Dual coding

> **TL;DR.** **Pair words with visuals.** Combining a verbal explanation with a relevant diagram, sketch, or spatial layout gives memory two routes to the same idea — and beats words alone. Make the visuals yourself; skip the decorative ones.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Paivio's **dual coding theory**: verbal and visual information are processed in partly separate channels, so encoding an idea *both* ways creates complementary traces and more retrieval paths. Mayer's research on **multimedia learning** confirms people learn better from **words + relevant pictures** than from words alone.

The big caveat is the **coherence/extraneous-load** principle: *relevant* visuals help; decorative or busy ones **hurt** by stealing working memory. A clean diagram of how a system works beats a stock photo of someone studying.

## How to apply

- **Draw it.** Turn processes into diagrams, timelines, flowcharts; sketch relationships rather than only writing prose.
- **Map concepts spatially** (concept maps, mind maps) so structure is visible — pairs naturally with [structure first](structure-first.md).
- **Make your own visuals** (the act of constructing them is itself good encoding) and keep them minimal and meaningful.
