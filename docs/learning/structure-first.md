# Structure first

> **TL;DR.** Build the **scaffold before the details.** Get the big-picture structure — the outline, the organizing principles, how the parts relate — *before* drilling into facts. New information sticks when there's already a framework (schema) to hang it on.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Memory isn't a pile of isolated facts; it's organized into **schemas** — structured frameworks. Ausubel's **advance organizers** showed that giving learners a high-level conceptual frame *before* detailed material improves comprehension and retention: the frame gives each new fact a place to attach.

It's also what separates experts from novices (*How People Learn*): experts don't just know more, they organize knowledge around **core principles and structure**, which makes retrieval and transfer easier. Learning a subject is largely **building and refining its schema**, not accumulating disconnected facts.

## How to apply

- **Do a structure pass first:** skim the table of contents / headings / summary to map the territory before reading deeply.
- **Make the scaffold explicit:** a one-page outline, a concept map, or "what are the 5 big ideas and how do they connect?"
- **Learn organizing principles before details** — the rules that generate the facts beat the facts themselves.
- Slot each new detail into the map: "where does this fit?"
