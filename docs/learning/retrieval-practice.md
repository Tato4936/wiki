# Retrieval practice (active recall)

> **TL;DR.** **Testing yourself is the single most powerful study technique.** Pulling information *out* of memory (closed-book recall, flashcards, practice questions) builds far more durable learning than putting it *in* again (rereading). It feels harder and less reassuring — and that's exactly why it works.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

The **testing effect**: the act of retrieving a memory strengthens it more than re-studying does. In Roediger & Karpicke (2006), students who studied then *tested* recalled ~50% more a week later than students who studied the same material repeatedly — even though the re-readers felt more confident. Karpicke & Blunt (2011, *Science*) found retrieval practice even beat elaborate concept-mapping.

Dunlosky et al.'s review rated **practice testing among the two highest-utility techniques** of all those studied. It works because effortful retrieval reconsolidates and re-routes the memory (a [desirable difficulty](desirable-difficulties.md)) — re-reading just re-exposes you to fluent text.

## How to apply

- **Close the book and recall.** After reading, write down everything you remember from a blank page ("brain dump"), *then* check.
- **Turn notes into questions, not summaries.** Flashcards (Anki), past papers, self-quizzing.
- **Retrieve before you feel ready** — struggling to recall (even failing, then checking) beats recognition.
- **Combine with spacing** → spaced retrieval is the highest-leverage combo in this whole section.
