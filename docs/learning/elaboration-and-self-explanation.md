# Elaboration & self-explanation

> **TL;DR.** Don't just take information in — **interrogate it.** Ask *why* it's true and *how* it connects to what you already know, and explain each step to yourself in your own words. Meaning-rich processing encodes far better than passive reading.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium (real effect; moderate utility) · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Two related, evidence-backed moves:

- **Elaborative interrogation** — asking and answering "*why* is this true?", forcing you to link new facts to prior knowledge.
- **Self-explanation** — explaining *how* you got an answer or *why* a step follows, as you work through material.

Both deepen encoding by building richer connections (the old "levels of processing" idea: meaning-based processing beats shallow processing). Dunlosky rated them **moderate utility** — solid and worth doing, a notch below testing and spacing. The popular **Feynman technique** (explain it simply, as if teaching a beginner, and hunt for the gaps) is a practical packaging of the same idea.

## How to apply

- **Ask "why?" and "how does this fit?"** as you read, and actually answer — out loud or on paper.
- **Teach it.** Explain the concept simply to an imaginary novice; the points where you stumble are exactly what you don't understand yet.
- **Connect new → known:** relate each idea to something you already grasp (analogies, examples).
