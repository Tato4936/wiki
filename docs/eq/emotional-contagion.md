# Emotional contagion

> **TL;DR.** Emotions spread. We automatically mimic others' expressions, postures, and tone, then *feel*
> our way into matching moods — often below awareness. It's the fast, primitive root of affective empathy,
> and it means your emotional state is partly the room's.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-03

## What it is

**Emotional contagion** is the tendency to automatically **mimic and synchronize** with others — facial
expressions, vocal tone, posture, movement — and, via feedback from that mimicry, to **converge
emotionally** with them. It's largely automatic and pre-reflective: you catch a smile, or tension, before
you decide to. Hatfield, Cacioppo & Rapson described it as a multi-step mimicry-and-feedback process.

It's the **bottom-up root of [affective empathy](empathy-types.md)** — before you cognitively understand
someone, you've already partly caught their state. It scales to groups (moods propagate through teams and
crowds) and across media.

## How to apply

- **Your mood is partly contagious input.** Curate who and what you're emotionally downstream of —
  proximity to chronic negativity or to calm both transfer.
- You can **deliberately project** the state you want a room to catch (calm, warmth) — contagion runs
  outward from you too.
- Knowing a feeling was *caught* rather than generated by your situation helps you not over-trust it — a
  [reappraisal](reappraisal-vs-suppression.md) hook.
