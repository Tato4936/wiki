# Social, complex & cross-cultural

> **TL;DR.** Other-directed feelings (*gratitude*, *envy*, *jealousy*, *compassion*, *schadenfreude*), mixed/flat states (*ambivalence*, *ennui*, *numbness*), and untranslatable words worth borrowing.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-08

Part of the [emotion vocabulary](index.md) lexicon for building [emotional granularity](../emotional-granularity.md). The **right-hand column** is the granularity payload — *what sets each word apart from its neighbours*.

## Social & comparison emotions

Feelings *about other people* and how you compare to them.

| Word | What it is | What sets it apart | Valence · arousal |
|------|-----------|--------------------|-------------------|
| **appreciation** | A mild, warm recognition of the value or kindness of someone or something. | Lighter and more cognitive than gratitude, which carries a felt sense of indebtedness; appreciation can be directed at qualities, not just received favors. | pos · low |
| **respect** | A considered regard for someone's worth, character, or achievements. | Cooler and more deliberate than admiration, which adds a glow of wonder; respect can be granted dutifully where admiration cannot be willed. | pos · low |
| **trust** | A settled confidence in another's reliability, honesty, or goodwill. | Unlike respect, which esteems someone's qualities, trust specifically expects them to act well toward you in the future. | pos · low |
| **sympathy** | A feeling of concern and sorrow for another's misfortune, viewed from the outside. | More detached than empathy, which actually feels with the other; sympathy feels for them without inhabiting their experience. | mixed · low |
| **pity** | Sorrow for another's suffering tinged with a sense of one's own better fortune. | Unlike compassion, which meets suffering as an equal and moves toward help, pity looks down from a position of relative superiority. | mixed · low |
| **gratitude** | A warm, thankful feeling toward someone who has benefited you. | Unlike appreciation, which can be diffuse esteem, gratitude is bound to a specific benefit received and carries a felt pull toward reciprocity. | pos · low |
| **empathy** | The felt sharing of another person's emotional state as if it were partly your own. | Closer and more immersive than sympathy; where compassion adds care and a wish to help, empathy is the raw resonance with what the other feels. | mixed · med |
| **compassion** | A caring concern for another's suffering coupled with the urge to relieve it. | Unlike empathy, which merely mirrors the feeling, compassion adds tenderness and a motivating push toward action; unlike pity, it carries no condescension. | mixed · med |
| **admiration** | A pleasurable regard mixed with wonder at someone's excellence or virtue. | Warmer and more uplifted than respect; admiration delights in another's superiority where envy would resent it. | pos · med |
| **disdain** | A cool feeling that someone or something is beneath your notice or worth. | More aloof and dismissive than contempt's settled verdict and scorn's open derision; disdain simply turns away as if the object is unworthy of engagement. | neg · low |
| **covetousness** | A craving desire to possess something that belongs to another. | Unlike envy, which resents the other for having it, covetousness fixes on wanting the thing itself rather than begrudging the person. | neg · med |
| **freudenfreude** | Warm pleasure taken in another person's success or good fortune. | The mirror opposite of envy and the social complement of empathy; where vicarious pride claims a share of the achievement, freudenfreude simply enjoys their win as theirs. | pos · med |
| **vicarious pride** | A swell of pride felt on behalf of someone else's accomplishment, through one's connection to them. | Unlike freudenfreude, which celebrates another's win as wholly theirs, vicarious pride claims a share of the glory through connection to the person. | pos · med |
| **vicarious embarrassment** | A cringing discomfort felt on behalf of someone making a fool of themselves. | Unlike vicarious pride or freudenfreude, which share in another's good moment, vicarious embarrassment is a self-conscious flush as if their social blunder were your own. | neg · med |
| **scorn** | An open, contemptuous rejection of someone as worthless or ridiculous. | More vocal and derisive than disdain's silent dismissal; scorn actively mocks where contempt is a colder, settled verdict. | neg · med |
| **gloating** | Smug, self-satisfied pleasure openly taken in one's own triumph or another's defeat. | Unlike schadenfreude, which can be a private inner glee, gloating is the outward display of relishing the win, often rubbed in the loser's face. | pos · med |
| **jealousy** | A fearful, possessive distress at the threat of losing someone or something to a rival. | Unlike envy, which wants what another has, jealousy guards what one already has against a perceived intruder. | neg · high |
| **schadenfreude** | A flash of pleasure at another person's misfortune or failure. | The dark twin of freudenfreude and the satisfied counterpart of envy; unlike gloating, it can be felt quietly without any triumph of one's own. | neg · med |
| **envy** | A painful, resentful longing aroused by another's advantage, possession, or quality. | Unlike covetousness, which simply wants the object, envy stings with comparison and ill will toward the person who has what you lack. | neg · high |
| **contempt** | A cold conviction that another person is base, inferior, or beneath respect. | More settled and condemnatory than disdain or scorn; where scorn erupts and disdain dismisses, contempt is a hardened verdict on the person's worth. | neg · low |
| **righteous indignation** | A burning anger on behalf of justice violated or wrong done to others. | Unlike contempt, which looks down on a person, righteous indignation is fired by a moral breach and feels justified rather than merely hostile. | neg · high |
| **belonging** | The warm sense of being accepted as a genuine part of a group or relationship. | Distinct from trust or appreciation; it is the felt security of inclusion and mattering to others. | pos · low |
| **feeling seen** | The relief and warmth of being accurately understood and recognized by another. | More specific than appreciation or trust; centers on one's inner self being perceived correctly. | pos · low |
| **kama muta** | The 'being moved' warmth of sudden communal connection, often with tears or chills. | A distinct heart-warming surge at moments of love/unity; more communal and sharper than generic being-moved. | pos · med |
| **social awkwardness** | The squirming discomfort of not knowing how to behave in an interaction. | Unlike embarrassment (after a misstep) it is anticipatory/ongoing uncertainty about social footing. | neg · med |
| **feeling like a burden** | Distress at believing one is imposing on or draining others. | A self-conscious social emotion distinct from guilt; about being a net cost rather than having done wrong. | neg · med |

## Complex, mixed & flat states

Blends, mixed feelings, and the flat or absent states.

| Word | What it is | What sets it apart | Valence · arousal |
|------|-----------|--------------------|-------------------|
| **indifference** | A mild absence of preference or care about a particular outcome, leaving you neither drawn toward nor away from it. | Milder and more outcome-specific than apathy, which is a broader inability to muster caring; indifference can coexist with active interest elsewhere. | neutral · low |
| **boredom** | A restless dissatisfaction arising when the present situation fails to engage you and nothing available seems worth doing. | Unlike apathy, boredom still wants stimulation and feels its absence as aversive; unlike ennui, it is situational and lifts once something interesting appears. | neg · low |
| **apathy** | A pervasive flatness in which motivation, interest, and emotional responsiveness are all dampened. | Broader and more global than indifference, which targets one thing, and more motivational than numbness, which is about not feeling rather than not caring. | neg · low |
| **malaise** | A diffuse, hard-to-locate sense that something is wrong or off, without a clear cause. | Vaguer and more pervasive than felt stress, which has identifiable pressure; unlike ennui, malaise carries unease rather than weary tedium. | neg · low |
| **ennui** | A weary, jaded listlessness in which everything feels stale and nothing seems worth caring about. | More existential and chronic than ordinary boredom, carrying world-weariness rather than a temporary lack of stimulation. | neg · low |
| **numbness** | A felt deadening in which emotions that should be present are muted or unreachable. | Unlike emptiness, which registers a felt void, numbness is the dulling of feeling itself; unlike apathy, it is about blocked affect rather than lost motivation. | neg · low |
| **emptiness** | An aching sense of inner void, as if something essential is missing from within. | Where numbness is feeling muffled, emptiness is the felt presence of absence; hollowness is its close cousin but emphasizes drained meaning rather than a void. | neg · low |
| **hollowness** | A sense of being drained of substance or meaning, going through motions that feel weightless. | Closely related to emptiness but stressing the loss of inner depth or significance, often after something that should have felt meaningful did not. | neg · low |
| **nostalgia** | A wistful, fond yearning for a remembered past that you know cannot return. | Unlike bittersweetness in general, nostalgia is specifically aimed at the past; its longing is gentle and affectionate rather than sharply painful. | mixed · low |
| **bittersweetness** | The simultaneous experience of pleasure and sorrow within a single moment or event. | More immediate and present-focused than nostalgia's backward gaze, and unlike plain ambivalence the two feelings are felt together rather than as competing pulls. | mixed · low |
| **ambivalence** | The state of holding opposing feelings or attitudes toward the same person, thing, or choice at once. | Broader and steadier than conflictedness, which sharpens into an active tension demanding resolution; ambivalence can sit unresolved without urgency. | mixed · low |
| **conflictedness** | An uneasy inner pull in which competing desires or values actively contend over a pending choice. | Sharper and more action-oriented than ambivalence, which can rest passively; conflictedness presses toward a resolution it cannot yet reach. | mixed · med |
| **restlessness** | An agitated inability to settle, with a pull toward movement or change you cannot quite direct. | More diffuse and lower-key than antsiness, which is sharper and more physically fidgety; restlessness can be mental and existential as well as bodily. | neg · med |
| **antsiness** | A jittery, fidgety impatience that makes staying still feel intolerable. | More acute and bodily than restlessness, concentrated in a near-term urge to move or act now rather than a vaguer unsettledness. | neg · med |
| **vulnerability** | The felt openness to being hurt, judged, or rejected that comes with lowering one's defenses. | Vulnerability is the inner sense of exposure to harm, and can be mixed because it also enables intimacy; exposure foregrounds being seen, the outward face of the same condition. | mixed · med |
| **exposure** | The uneasy sense of being seen, watched, or laid bare before others without protection. | Where vulnerability is openness to being hurt, exposure foregrounds being visible and unable to hide; it shades toward shame when what is seen feels shameful. | neg · med |
| **existential longing** | A deep, unfulfillable yearning for meaning, connection, or a home one cannot name. | Unlike nostalgia, it reaches toward something never possessed rather than a lost past, and its ache is metaphysical rather than tied to any specific memory. | mixed · med |
| **felt stress** | The pressured, taxed sense of demands exceeding the resources you feel you have to meet them. | Unlike overwhelm, stress can still feel manageable and goal-directed; overwhelm is the point at which that pressure floods past your capacity to cope. | neg · high |
| **anxious hope** | An expectant reaching toward a wished-for outcome shadowed by dread that it may not come. | Unlike pure anxiety, a genuine positive pull is present; unlike plain hope, the upward expectation is braided with anticipatory fear of disappointment. | mixed · high |
| **overwhelm** | The flooded, swamped sense that demands or emotions have surged past what you can process or handle. | The high-arousal endpoint past felt stress: where stress is strained coping, overwhelm is coping breaking down under the sheer volume. | neg · high |
| **anhedonia** | The flat inability to feel pleasure in things normally enjoyable. | Distinct from numbness (absence of feeling broadly); specifically the loss of capacity for pleasure. | neg · low |
| **poignancy** | A bittersweet pang of meaning, often when joy and loss coexist in one moment. | Finer than bittersweetness; a brief acute ache of beauty-touched-by-transience. | mixed · low |
| **dissociation** | A detached, unreal feeling of being outside oneself or that the world is filmlike. | Distinct from numbness; involves disrupted sense of reality and self-presence, not just dulled affect. | neg · low |

## Cross-cultural / untranslatable

Precise feelings other languages named and English never did.

| Word | What it is | What sets it apart | Valence · arousal |
|------|-----------|--------------------|-------------------|
| **gemütlichkeit** | A German word for the warm, cozy sense of belonging and unhurried ease that arises in a snug, congenial setting among friendly company. | Unlike Danish hygge, which centers on the deliberate aesthetic of coziness itself, gemütlichkeit emphasizes the social warmth and good-natured conviviality of the people present; gezelligheid is its closest Dutch cousin but leans more toward sociable togetherness than physical snugness. | pos · low |
| **hygge** | A Danish word for the contented, intimate coziness of a simple pleasure savored deliberately, like candlelight and a warm drink on a dark evening. | Where gemütlichkeit foregrounds congenial company, hygge can be solitary and is more about cultivating a soothing atmosphere; it lacks the lively sociability that defines Dutch gezelligheid. | pos · low |
| **gezelligheid** | A Dutch word for the cozy, convivial warmth of pleasant togetherness shared in good company, whether at a cafe table or a crowded living room. | Unlike hygge, which can be enjoyed alone, gezelligheid is fundamentally social and requires others; it is livelier and more outgoing than the still, hushed snugness of gemütlichkeit. | pos · low |
| **waldeinsamkeit** | A German word for the serene, slightly solemn feeling of being alone in the woods, sensing one's smallness amid the quiet of nature. | It differs from ordinary solitude by being specifically forest-bound and faintly transcendent rather than lonely; against fernweh's restless craving to roam, waldeinsamkeit is a settled stillness in a place already reached. | pos · low |
| **mono no aware** | A Japanese phrase for the gentle, bittersweet poignancy of being moved by the transience of things, like cherry blossoms that must soon fall. | Unlike Portuguese saudade, which aches for a specific absent person or place, mono no aware is a diffuse tenderness toward impermanence itself; it is quieter and more accepting than the yearning of sehnsucht. | mixed · low |
| **han** | A Korean word for a profound, enduring sorrow and resentment born of accumulated suffering, injustice, and unresolved grief, often felt collectively. | Unlike acute grief that passes, han is a deep, sedimented ache carried over a lifetime or generations, fused with simmering injustice; it is heavier and more lasting than the personal longing of saudade. | neg · med |
| **saudade** | A Portuguese word for the deep, melancholic longing for someone or something absent and beloved, suffused with the bittersweet pleasure of remembering. | Unlike plain nostalgia, saudade carries a love-soaked ache for a specific absence and may mourn what never was; against sehnsucht's diffuse spiritual yearning, saudade is anchored to a concrete person, place, or time. | mixed · med |
| **sehnsucht** | A German word for an intense, almost spiritual yearning for an unattainable or ill-defined ideal, a homesickness for somewhere that may not exist. | Where saudade longs for something concrete once possessed, sehnsucht reaches toward a vague, transcendent perfection that was never had; it is more metaphysical and diffuse than fernweh's longing for actual distant places. | mixed · med |
| **fernweh** | A German word for the aching longing to be somewhere far away, a wanderlust felt as homesickness for places one has never seen. | It is the mirror of homesickness, pulling outward toward the distant rather than back toward home; unlike sehnsucht's vague metaphysical yearning, fernweh's object is concrete travel and faraway places. | mixed · med |
| **dépaysement** | A French word for the disorienting yet often exhilarating feeling of being out of one's own country, unmoored from familiar surroundings. | It contrasts with fernweh, which is the longing to leave before departure; dépaysement is the actual sensation of displacement once away, mixing unease with the thrill of strangeness rather than craving distance. | mixed · med |
| **koi no yokan** | A Japanese phrase for the premonition, on first meeting someone, that the two of you are destined to fall in love. | It is not love at first sight but the sense that love is inevitable later; against forelsket's present euphoria, koi no yokan is anticipatory and quieter, a forecast rather than the storm itself. | pos · med |
| **iktsuarpok** | An Inuit (Inuktitut) word for the restless anticipation that makes you keep going outside to check whether an expected visitor has arrived. | Unlike general impatience, iktsuarpok is bound to awaiting a specific person and expresses itself in repeated physical checking; it is more agitated and outward than the inward longing of saudade. | mixed · med |
| **mamihlapinatapai** | A Yaghan word for the charged, wordless look shared between two people who both desire something but each waits for the other to act first. | It differs from ordinary hesitation by being mutual and unspoken, a shared electric standoff of wanting; against koi no yokan's solitary premonition, mamihlapinatapai requires two people locked in reciprocal expectation. | mixed · med |
| **forelsket** | A Norwegian word for the giddy, euphoric state of newly falling in love, when everything about the beloved feels luminous. | Unlike koi no yokan, the Japanese premonition that love is coming, forelsket is the intoxication of love already arrived; it is the bright early rush, not the deeper attachment that follows. | pos · high |
| **gigil** | A Tagalog word for the overwhelming, almost aggressive urge to squeeze or pinch something unbearably cute or beloved. | Unlike simple affection, gigil is a near-violent overflow of tenderness that grits the teeth and clenches the hands; it is more bodily and explosive than the soft warmth of forelsket. | pos · high |
| **torschlusspanik** | A German word, literally 'gate-shut panic,' for the anxious dread that time is running out and life's opportunities are closing before you have seized them. | Unlike ordinary worry about the future, torschlusspanik is the specific urgency of a narrowing window, often around age or milestones; it is far more activated and fearful than the wistful longing of saudade. | neg · high |
| **litost** | A Czech word for the agonized state of sudden insight into one's own misery, a tormented blend of grief, self-pity, and the impulse for revenge. | Unlike simple sorrow, litost combusts when humiliation lays bare one's wretchedness and lashes outward in spite; it is sharper and more self-wounding than the gentle melancholy of saudade. | neg · high |
| **tarab** | An Arabic word for the rapturous, transported ecstasy induced by music, a state of enchantment in which the listener is swept beyond themselves. | Unlike ordinary musical enjoyment, tarab is an intense trance-like exaltation that fuses performer and audience; it is far more aroused and euphoric than the quiet poignancy of mono no aware. | pos · high |
| **sisu** | A Finnish word for stoic determination and grit to persist against overwhelming odds. | More extreme and adversity-bound than ordinary determination; a cultural ideal of inner fortitude. | pos · med |
| **jeong** | A Korean word for deep affectionate attachment and connectedness built up between people over time. | Untranslatable bond stronger than fondness; a sticky relational glue not requiring liking. | pos · low |
| **ubuntu** | A Nguni Bantu word for the warm sense that one's humanity is bound up in the humanity of others. | A communal feeling of shared personhood distinct from belonging; 'I am because we are'. | pos · low |
| **toska** | A Russian word for a great spiritual anguish and restless yearning, often without identifiable cause. | Deeper and vaguer than melancholy; an aching longing of the soul described by Nabokov as having no English equal. | neg · low |
| **mbuki-mvuki** | A Bantu word for the irrepressible urge to shed clothes and dance freely with joy. | An embodied joyful impulse with no English single word; specific kinetic exuberance. | pos · high |
