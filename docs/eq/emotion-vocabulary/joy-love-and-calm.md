# Joy, love & calm

> **TL;DR.** The positives, split three ways: active *joy*, low-arousal *calm/contentment* (the usual blind spot), and *love/affection/desire*.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-08

Part of the [emotion vocabulary](index.md) lexicon for building [emotional granularity](../emotional-granularity.md). The **right-hand column** is the granularity payload — *what sets each word apart from its neighbours*.

## Joy & happiness

Active pleasure, from a light mood to ecstasy.

| Word | What it is | What sets it apart | Valence · arousal |
|------|-----------|--------------------|-------------------|
| **lightheartedness** | A relaxed, carefree feeling of being free from worry or heaviness. | More about the absence of burden than the active positive brightness of cheerfulness; unlike amusement it needs no playful trigger. | pos · low |
| **gladness** | A quiet, warm pleasure at something that has happened or is the case. | Gentler and more circumstantial than joy, and less buoyant than cheerfulness; a settled 'I'm glad' rather than an animated high spirit. | pos · low |
| **cheerfulness** | A bright, outwardly visible disposition of good spirits and optimism. | More sustained and outward than fleeting gladness, and more grounded than buoyancy, which carries a sense of bouncing lift. | pos · med |
| **buoyancy** | A light, rising sense of being uplifted and resilient in spirit. | Adds a feeling of lift and effortless rebound that plain cheerfulness lacks, but is steadier and less acute than exhilaration. | pos · med |
| **amusement** | The pleasant feeling of being entertained or tickled by something funny or charming. | Specifically tied to finding something humorous or diverting, whereas mirth is its fuller overflow into laughter and enjoyment is broader and not humor-bound. | pos · med |
| **mirth** | Lively, expressive merriment that bubbles up into laughter and gaiety. | More social and laughter-filled than quiet amusement, and warmer and more good-natured than glee, which can carry a gloating edge. | pos · med |
| **enjoyment** | The satisfying pleasure taken in an activity or experience as it unfolds. | Broader and more process-oriented than pleasure, which can be a momentary sensory hit, and calmer than delight, which spikes with surprise. | pos · med |
| **pleasure** | An agreeable feeling of gratification arising from something liked or desired. | More immediate and often sensory than sustained enjoyment, and broader and less sparkling than delight. | pos · med |
| **delight** | A keen, lively pleasure that lights up in response to something charming or pleasing. | Sharper and more reactive than steady enjoyment, and gentler and more refined than the loud burst of glee. | pos · med |
| **joy** | A deep, expansive feeling of happiness and gladness about something cherished. | Fuller and more wholehearted than delight, and warmer and more enduring than glee, which is sharper and more excitable. | pos · med |
| **glee** | A keen, often barely containable delight that spills over into excitement. | More excited and impish than dignified joy, sometimes carrying a triumphant or mischievous edge that mirth's good-natured laughter does not. | pos · high |
| **giddiness** | A flighty, almost intoxicated elation that leaves one silly and unable to settle. | Carries a whirling loss of composure and frivolity that glee lacks, yet is sillier and less triumphant than jubilation. | pos · high |
| **exhilaration** | An invigorating thrill of heightened energy and elation, often from intense activity. | More charged with physical aliveness and stimulation than warm joy, and more grounded in a triggering rush than free-floating elation. | pos · high |
| **jubilation** | Triumphant, exultant rejoicing, typically expressed openly and often shared. | More celebratory and victory-tinged than private elation, and more outwardly demonstrative than the inner uplift of euphoria. | pos · high |
| **elation** | A buoyant high of great joy and self-assured exuberance lifting one's spirits. | More personal and pride-adjacent than communal jubilation, and a notch below the all-consuming, sometimes unmoored intensity of euphoria. | pos · high |
| **euphoria** | An intense, sweeping state of overwhelming happiness and well-being. | More totalizing and free-floating than situational elation, and more diffuse and bodily than rapture, which fixes on an object of awe. | pos · high |
| **rapture** | An intense, transported joy that seizes and absorbs one in delight or awe. | More fixated and reverent toward its object than diffuse euphoria, and slightly more conscious than the self-dissolving abandon of ecstasy. | pos · high |
| **ecstasy** | An overpowering, trance-like joy so intense it transcends ordinary awareness. | The most extreme and self-transcending of the family, surpassing rapture in its loss of self and exceeding euphoria's bodily glow with near-mystical absorption. | pos · high |
| **playfulness** | A light, spontaneous readiness to joke, tease, or treat things non-seriously for fun. | Unlike amusement (a reaction to something funny), playfulness is a proactive disposition to generate levity. | pos · med |

## Contentment, calm & relief

Low-arousal good feelings — the quiet states people name least.

| Word | What it is | What sets it apart | Valence · arousal |
|------|-----------|--------------------|-------------------|
| **ease** | A light, unforced feeling that nothing is pressing on you and no effort is required. | Ease centers on the absence of strain or demand, whereas comfort centers on positive bodily or emotional well-being and contentment on durable satisfaction. | pos · low |
| **comfort** | The pleasant feeling of being at rest and free from distress, want, or pain. | Comfort emphasizes the relief of discomfort and a positive sense of well-being, whereas ease emphasizes mere lack of effort and coziness adds enclosed physical warmth. | pos · low |
| **coziness** | A warm, snug feeling of being sheltered in safe, pleasant surroundings. | Coziness adds a specifically physical, enclosed warmth that general comfort does not require, and is more situational than the inner steadiness of calm. | pos · low |
| **relaxation** | The feeling of tension draining out of body and mind, leaving you loose and unguarded. | Relaxation is the active loosening of held tension, whereas calm is the steady state once tension is gone and ease is the absence of effort rather than the release of strain. | pos · low |
| **mellowness** | A soft, easygoing, gently good-humored mood in which nothing feels worth getting worked up about. | Mellowness carries a faintly indulgent, low-key warmth that calm lacks, and is a passing mood rather than the durable satisfaction of contentment. | pos · low |
| **soothedness** | The gentled feeling of having been calmed after distress, with the agitation now smoothed away. | Soothedness implies a prior upset that something has eased, unlike calm or ease which need no preceding disturbance, and it stresses the gentling of agitation rather than the lifting of a burden as in relief. | pos · low |
| **reassurance** | The settled feeling that a doubt or worry has been answered, so it is safe to stop fearing. | Reassurance specifically dissolves a particular uncertainty or apprehension, whereas soothedness calms general agitation and security is a standing condition rather than the answering of one worry. | pos · low |
| **safety** | The felt sense of being protected and beyond harm's reach, so the guard can come down. | Felt safety is freedom from threat in the moment, whereas security adds the durable confidence that this protection will last and groundedness is inner stability rather than external protection. | pos · low |
| **security** | A confident, settled feeling that one is safe and that this safety is dependable and ongoing. | Security adds duration and trust to momentary felt safety, and unlike reassurance it is a standing condition rather than the answering of a specific worry. | pos · low |
| **calm** | A steady, undisturbed state in which the mind is quiet and the emotions are still. | Calm is the broad baseline of stillness, while placidity adds an unruffled smoothness and serenity adds a luminous, accepting clarity. | pos · low |
| **placidity** | A smooth, unruffled calm in which little disturbs the surface of one's mood. | Placidity stresses the absence of ripples or agitation, whereas calm is general quiet and tranquility adds a deeper, more enveloping stillness. | pos · low |
| **groundedness** | A stable, centered feeling of being rooted in oneself and the present moment. | Groundedness is inner stability and self-possession, whereas security comes from external safety and equanimity is the evenness of mood held under pressure. | pos · low |
| **contentment** | A quiet satisfaction in which what one has feels enough and nothing more is wanted. | Contentment is the gentle gladness of sufficiency, whereas calm is mere stillness and peace is the deeper resolution of all inner conflict. | pos · low |
| **tranquility** | A deep, pervasive calm in which the whole self feels quiet and undisturbed. | Tranquility is fuller and more enveloping than ordinary calm, and unlike serenity it carries no connotation of clarity or transcendence, only profound stillness. | pos · low |
| **equanimity** | A composed evenness of mind that holds steady through ups, downs, and provocations. | Equanimity is balance actively maintained against disturbance, whereas tranquility is calm in the absence of disturbance and groundedness is rootedness rather than even-keeled response. | pos · low |
| **serenity** | A clear-minded, luminous calm marked by deep acceptance and inner light. | Serenity adds clarity and a radiant, accepting quality to tranquility, and is more elevated and spiritual than the everyday quiet of calm. | pos · low |
| **peace** | A profound, whole feeling that all conflict has resolved and one is wholly at rest within. | Peace is the most complete and resolved of these states, the felt end of all inner strife, whereas serenity stresses clarity and tranquility stresses stillness rather than resolution. | pos · low |
| **relief** | The sudden lightening felt when a threat, pain, or burden lifts away. | Relief is the most activated of this family, a brief surge of release tied to a specific stressor ending, unlike the steady low hum of calm or the slow gentling of soothedness. | pos · med |
| **savoring** | The active, attentive prolonging and heightening of a present pleasant experience. | Unlike contentment (a settled state) or pleasure (the raw feeling), savoring is the deliberate cognitive act of dwelling on and amplifying a good moment. | pos · low |
| **languor** | A pleasant heavy drowsiness or dreamy lassitude, often after exertion, warmth, or satiation. | Unlike relaxation (release of tension) or mellowness, languor carries a sensual, almost weighted sleepiness and reluctance to move. | pos · low |

## Love, affection & desire

Warmth, attachment and desire toward others.

| Word | What it is | What sets it apart | Valence · arousal |
|------|-----------|--------------------|-------------------|
| **fondness** | A gentle, settled liking and warm goodwill toward someone or something familiar. | Milder and more casual than affection; it is liking warmed by familiarity, lacking the deeper attachment or protective tenderness of stronger bonds. | pos · low |
| **warmth** | A diffuse, kindly glow of friendly feeling toward another person. | Broader and less targeted than fondness, which singles out a particular object; warmth is the general affectionate climate rather than a specific attachment. | pos · low |
| **affection** | A steady feeling of caring attachment and liking for someone. | Warmer and more committed than fondness but cooler and less protective than tenderness; it names the bond itself rather than the gentleness of caring or the heat of desire. | pos · low |
| **tenderness** | A soft, gentle, protective warmth that wells up toward someone vulnerable or beloved. | Differs from affection by its gentleness and protective impulse; where caring acts, tenderness is the melting softness behind the act. | pos · low |
| **caring** | An attached concern for another's wellbeing that disposes one to look after them. | More active and welfare-directed than affection, which is mostly a feeling; caring couples warm feeling with felt responsibility, without tenderness's melting softness. | pos · low |
| **sentimentality** | A tender, wistful softening of feeling stirred by reminders of loved people, places, or times. | Unlike affection toward a present person, sentimentality is affection refracted through memory and nostalgia, and can tip into indulgent or saccharine excess. | mixed · low |
| **attraction** | A felt pull of interest and appeal drawing one toward another person. | Unlike affection, which presupposes a bond, attraction is the initial magnetic draw, often physical or romantic, before any attachment has formed. | pos · med |
| **attachment** | A deep felt bond of closeness and dependence on another person for security. | Broader and more enduring than affection; where affection is warm liking, attachment is the felt need for the other's presence and the distress at separation. | pos · med |
| **protectiveness** | A warm, vigilant urge to shield a loved one from harm. | Sharper and more aroused than tenderness, which is soft and passive; protectiveness adds a guarding, defensive readiness on the beloved's behalf. | pos · med |
| **adoration** | A reverent, worshipful love that holds the beloved as wonderful and above oneself. | Unlike affection or tenderness, adoration looks up at its object with awe and idealization, closer to worship than to companionable warmth. | pos · med |
| **romantic longing** | An aching, yearning pull toward a beloved who is absent or out of reach. | Unlike desire's forward appetite, longing is colored by distance and lack, carrying a bittersweet ache rather than anticipation of satisfaction. | mixed · med |
| **devotion** | A profound, enduring commitment of love and loyalty that orients one's life around the beloved. | Steadier and more self-giving than passion's blaze; where passion flares with arousal, devotion is the deep, sustained dedication that outlasts intensity. | pos · med |
| **desire** | A wanting, appetitive pull toward union with or closeness to someone. | More charged and goal-directed than attraction's mere pull; desire is the active craving for closeness or fulfillment, broader than purely physical lust. | pos · high |
| **infatuation** | An intense, often short-lived passion in which one is captivated and dazzled by another. | Hotter and more idealizing than attraction but shallower and more fragile than love; infatuation runs on fantasy and tends to burn out as reality intrudes. | mixed · high |
| **lust** | A strong, urgent craving for sexual gratification with another. | Narrower and more bodily than desire, which can be emotional or romantic; lust is desire stripped to physical appetite, without adoration's reverence or devotion's loyalty. | pos · high |
| **limerence** | An obsessive, involuntary infatuation marked by intrusive thoughts and an acute craving for the other's reciprocation. | Unlike ordinary infatuation, limerence is compulsive and tormenting, dominated by uncertainty and a desperate hunger for any sign of being wanted back. | mixed · high |
| **passion** | An overpowering surge of intense love and desire that consumes attention and energy. | More all-engulfing and emotionally hot than lust's bodily focus; passion fuses craving with strong feeling, sweeping the whole person rather than just the senses. | pos · high |
| **compersion** | Joy felt at a loved one's pleasure or happiness, including in another relationship. | The loving counterpart of jealousy; unlike freudenfreude (joy at anyone's success) it is rooted in intimate attachment. | pos · med |
| **agape** | Selfless, unconditional, all-embracing benevolent love not contingent on reciprocity. | Distinct from affection or devotion in its impartial, giving, often spiritual character that does not seek return. | pos · low |
| **storge** | The steady, familiar affection that grows through long familiarity, as among family. | Unlike infatuation or passion, storge is undramatic, habitual fondness built by time and shared life. | pos · low |
