# Anger & disgust

> **TL;DR.** The hot and the recoiling negatives — tell *irritation* from *resentment* from *contempt*, and *distaste* from *revulsion*.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-08

Part of the [emotion vocabulary](index.md) lexicon for building [emotional granularity](../emotional-granularity.md). The **right-hand column** is the granularity payload — *what sets each word apart from its neighbours*.

## Anger & irritation

From mild bother to explosive loss of control.

| Word | What it is | What sets it apart | Valence · arousal |
|------|-----------|--------------------|-------------------|
| **displeasure** | A mild feeling of disapproval or dissatisfaction when something fails to meet your standards or wishes. | Cooler and more evaluative than annoyance or irritation: it is reserved disapproval rather than a prickly, agitated bother. | neg · low |
| **annoyance** | A small, passing displeasure at something mildly bothersome or inconvenient. | Lighter and more incidental than irritation, which carries a persistent grating quality; lacks the considered disapproval of displeasure. | neg · low |
| **irritation** | A prickly, low-grade agitation caused by something that keeps grating on you. | More abrasive and bodily than annoyance, yet milder and more fleeting than anger; it builds from ongoing friction rather than a single offense. | neg · med |
| **impatience** | Restless irritation at having to wait or at things moving too slowly. | Specifically tied to delay and the urge to hurry, with an edge of eagerness, rather than the diffuse bother of general irritation. | neg · med |
| **aggravation** | A worsened, intensified irritation that has been stoked by repetition or escalation. | Implies the bother has compounded and grown heavier over time, sitting above plain irritation but still short of full anger. | neg · med |
| **frustration** | The agitated distress of being blocked, thwarted, or unable to achieve a goal. | Centers on obstruction of a goal rather than mere bother, and is aimed at a situation as much as at any person. | neg · med |
| **exasperation** | The fed-up irritation that arises when your patience has finally been worn out. | Adds to frustration the sense of patience exhausted by repeated trying, and is sharper and more vocal than simple irritation. | neg · med |
| **pique** | A flash of wounded irritation triggered by a slight to one's pride or vanity. | Springs specifically from bruised ego and tends to be brief and petulant, rather than the sustained grating of irritation. | neg · med |
| **offense** | The stung, hurt anger of feeling insulted or disrespected by someone's words or actions. | Relational and centered on a perceived insult to oneself, blending personal injury with anger rather than the goal-blockage of frustration. | neg · med |
| **indignation** | Righteous anger at something perceived as unfair, unjust, or unworthy. | Morally charged and outward-looking, fueled by violated fairness rather than the personal-insult focus of offense. | neg · high |
| **resentment** | A simmering, held-onto anger at being wronged, slighted, or treated unfairly. | Slow-burning and harbored over time, unlike indignation's hot, immediate moral flare. | neg · med |
| **bitterness** | A sour, embittered anger born of accumulated hurt, disappointment, or grievance. | Deeper and more corrosive than resentment, it has soured into a settled outlook that colors how one sees the world. | neg · low |
| **hostility** | An aggressive, antagonistic anger that turns one against another as an adversary. | Outwardly combative and oriented toward opposition or attack, unlike the inward, private hold of resentment. | neg · high |
| **anger** | The strong feeling of displeasure and antagonism aroused by a wrong, threat, or frustration. | The general high-intensity emotion of the family; fuller and more forceful than irritation, but more controllable and less explosive than rage or fury. | neg · high |
| **outrage** | Intense, shocked anger at something felt to be grossly wrong or offensive. | Hotter and more explosive than indignation, reacting to an outright violation with a sense of moral shock rather than measured disapproval. | neg · high |
| **seething** | A state of barely contained anger boiling intensely just beneath the surface. | The fury is suppressed and inwardly churning rather than discharged, unlike the open eruption of rage. | neg · high |
| **wrath** | Powerful, vengeful anger that seeks to punish or retaliate. | Graver and more retributive than ordinary anger, carrying an intent to inflict justice or harm that rage and fury lack. | neg · high |
| **fury** | Wild, violent anger of overwhelming force. | More frenzied and chaotic than wrath's purposeful retribution, with a near-mad ferocity. | neg · high |
| **rage** | Uncontrollable, explosive anger that overwhelms reason and self-control. | The most consuming form here, bursting outward in a loss of control; exceeds the focused force of anger and emphasizes eruptive discharge over fury's chaotic frenzy. | neg · high |
| **cold anger** | Cold anger laced with looking down on its target as beneath respect. | Unlike hot rage, it is chilled and dismissive, blending anger with disdain. | neg · low |
| **defensiveness** | Reactive irritation and guardedness when one feels criticized or threatened. | A protective anger-adjacent stance distinct from indignation; aimed at warding off perceived attack. | neg · med |

## Disgust & aversion

Recoil and rejection — sensory first, then moral.

| Word | What it is | What sets it apart | Valence · arousal |
|------|-----------|--------------------|-------------------|
| **distaste** | A mild sense that something is unpleasant or not to one's liking, prompting a soft inclination to turn away. | Distaste is the gentlest aversion, registering 'I'd rather not' rather than the active rejection of dislike or the visceral churn of disgust. | neg · low |
| **dislike** | A settled negative feeling toward something or someone, the simple wish that it were not there. | Dislike is broader and more attitudinal than distaste, and unlike revulsion it carries no bodily recoil — it is an orientation that leans away rather than a gut that flinches. | neg · low |
| **aversion** | A persistent felt pull to avoid or stay away from something one finds objectionable. | Aversion emphasizes the motivational withdrawal — the steady wanting-to-avoid — more than the sensory yuck of disgust or the cool judgment of dislike. | neg · med |
| **the ick** | A sudden wave of turn-off toward someone you were attracted to, triggered by a small offputting detail. | Uniquely interpersonal and romantic, 'the ick' flips desire into aversion in an instant over a trivial cue, where ordinary distaste needs no prior attraction and builds no such reversal. | neg · high |
| **grossed-out** | The casual, everyday feeling of being icked by something yucky or unhygienic. | Being grossed-out is the colloquial, lower-stakes cousin of disgust — momentary and reactive, lacking the gravity of repugnance or the moral weight of abhorrence. | neg · med |
| **moral disgust** | The disgust-flavored condemnation felt toward acts or ideas perceived as ethically depraved or polluting. | Moral disgust borrows the body's rejection response and aims it at wrongdoing rather than physical filth, recoiling from the deed as tainted rather than from a mere object that turns the stomach. | neg · med |
| **repugnance** | A strong feeling that something is offensive and fundamentally unacceptable to one's sensibilities. | Repugnance is more emphatic and principled than plain disgust, framing the object as something one cannot countenance rather than merely something that turns the stomach. | neg · med |
| **disgust** | The core rejecting emotion in which something foul, contaminating, or offensive provokes a wish to expel or get away from it. | Disgust is the prototypical center of this family — more visceral and contamination-focused than dislike, yet less extreme than the overwhelming bodily heave of revulsion. | neg · high |
| **repulsion** | A forceful felt push away from something experienced as physically or morally offensive. | Repulsion stresses the propulsive 'get-it-away-from-me' force more than the inner churn of disgust, and it is more acute and immediate than slow-burning loathing. | neg · high |
| **revulsion** | A sudden, violent surge of disgust that grips the whole body and makes one recoil. | Revulsion is disgust at its most overwhelming and physical — a sharp involuntary heave that outstrips the steadier repugnance and the more attitudinal repulsion. | neg · high |
| **loathing** | An intense, enduring detestation that fuses deep disgust with active hatred of its object. | Loathing adds sustained hostility and longevity that a momentary spasm of revulsion lacks, smoldering as settled hatred rather than flaring and fading. | neg · high |
| **abhorrence** | A profound, total repudiation of something felt as utterly detestable and not to be tolerated. | Abhorrence is the most absolute and morally charged term in the family, conveying a complete principled rejection that goes beyond even loathing's heated hatred into categorical refusal. | neg · high |
