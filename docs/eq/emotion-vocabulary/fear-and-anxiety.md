# Fear & anxiety

> **TL;DR.** The threat family, faint *unease* through full *terror* — and the slow burn of *worry*, *dread*, and *foreboding*.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-08

Part of the [emotion vocabulary](index.md) lexicon for building [emotional granularity](../emotional-granularity.md). The **right-hand column** is the granularity payload — *what sets each word apart from its neighbours*.

## Fear & anxiety

Threat and uncertainty, from faint unease to terror.

| Word | What it is | What sets it apart | Valence · arousal |
|------|-----------|--------------------|-------------------|
| **unease** | A vague, low-level feeling that something is not quite right, without an identifiable cause. | Milder and more diffuse than worry, which fixes on a specific problem; unease lacks an identifiable object. | neg · low |
| **disquiet** | A persistent, restless sense of being troubled that disturbs one's inner calm. | More inward and brooding than unease, and more enduring than a passing nervous moment; it unsettles rather than spikes. | neg · low |
| **apprehension** | A mild fearful expectation that something unpleasant is about to happen. | More clearly forward-looking and object-focused than unease, but calmer and less intense than dread or trepidation. | neg · med |
| **worry** | Repetitive anxious thinking about a specific anticipated problem or threat. | More cognitive and rumination-driven than anxiety's bodily charge, and more sharply targeted than free-floating unease. | neg · med |
| **nervousness** | A jumpy, keyed-up state of mild fearful anticipation, often before an event. | More bodily and situational than worry's mental looping, and lighter than the chronic tension of anxiety. | neg · med |
| **edginess** | An irritable, on-edge tension in which one feels easily startled or provoked. | Blends fear-arousal with irritability, unlike pure nervousness, and is more reactive and prickly than steady worry. | neg · med |
| **anxiety** | A sustained state of apprehensive tension about uncertain or future threats. | Broader and more enduring than worry, and more diffuse than fear, which targets a present, identifiable danger. | neg · med |
| **anticipatory anxiety** | Apprehensive tension that builds in advance of a specific feared upcoming event. | Unlike general anxiety, it is anchored to a known future moment and tends to subside once that moment arrives or passes. | neg · med |
| **trepidation** | Hesitant, tremulous fear felt when approaching something one must face. | More acute and approach-focused than apprehension, yet more restrained and anticipatory than full dread. | neg · med |
| **foreboding** | A heavy, ominous sense that something bad is imminent. | Darker and more certain-feeling than apprehension, carrying a premonitory weight that mere worry lacks. | neg · med |
| **performance anxiety** | Fearful tension arising from the prospect of being evaluated while acting in front of others. | A social-evaluative subtype of anticipatory anxiety, centered specifically on the threat of judgment or failure before an audience. | neg · high |
| **dread** | Intense reluctant fear of an anticipated event one feels powerless to avoid. | Heavier and more anticipatory than fear of a present danger, and more inescapable-feeling than foreboding. | neg · high |
| **alarm** | A sudden burst of fearful arousal triggered by an abruptly perceived danger. | Sharper and more sudden than anxiety, but less engulfing than fright or panic; it is the initial jolt of threat detection. | neg · high |
| **fright** | A sharp, sudden shock of fear in response to something startling. | More abrupt and short-lived than sustained fear, and more reflexive than alarm, which can keep one alert afterward. | neg · high |
| **fear** | The emotional response to a present, identifiable threat to one's safety. | Unlike anxiety's diffuse anticipation, fear has a clear object in the here-and-now; it is the baseline term the others intensify or qualify. | neg · high |
| **panic** | A sudden, overwhelming surge of fear that overrides reason and self-control. | More acute and incapacitating than fear, marked by a loss of composure that ordinary fright does not entail. | neg · high |
| **terror** | Extreme, paralyzing fear in the face of severe, imminent danger. | More intense and engulfing than panic, often freezing rather than mobilizing the person. | neg · high |
| **horror** | An overwhelming dread mixed with revulsion at something monstrous or appalling. | Unlike terror's pure self-protective fear, horror fuses fear with disgust and is often evoked by witnessing something abhorrent. | neg · high |
| **paranoia** | Pervasive suspicious fear that others intend harm or are watching. | Unlike worry (about events) it is directed at malign intentions of others, often unfounded. | neg · high |
| **separation anxiety** | Fearful distress at the prospect or fact of being apart from an attachment figure. | Distinct from generalized anxiety; specifically tied to loss of proximity to a bonded other. | neg · high |
