# Shame, guilt & pride

> **TL;DR.** The self-evaluating emotions: *guilt* (a bad deed) vs *shame* (a bad self) vs *embarrassment* — and *pride* through its dark side, *hubris*.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-08

Part of the [emotion vocabulary](index.md) lexicon for building [emotional granularity](../emotional-granularity.md). The **right-hand column** is the granularity payload — *what sets each word apart from its neighbours*.

## Shame, guilt & embarrassment

The self under judgment — about a *deed* (guilt) or the *self* (shame).

| Word | What it is | What sets it apart | Valence · arousal |
|------|-----------|--------------------|-------------------|
| **self-consciousness** | A heightened, uneasy awareness of oneself as the object of others' attention or judgment. | Milder and more diffuse than embarrassment; it is the background sense of being watched rather than the acute sting of a specific slip. | neg · low |
| **sheepishness** | A mild, faintly comic feeling of having been caught in a small fault or folly. | Lighter and more rueful than embarrassment, often paired with a smile; abashment is more silenced and deferential, while sheepishness retains a self-aware wryness. | neg · low |
| **abashment** | A subdued loss of composure and confidence under another's gaze or disapproval. | Quieter and more deflating than embarrassment; where embarrassment flushes and fidgets, abashment shrinks and falls silent. | neg · low |
| **self-reproach** | A reproving inner judgment directed at oneself for a fault or failure. | More cognitive and verbal than guilt's emotional ache, and milder than remorse; it is the inner voice scolding rather than the heavy felt sense of wrongdoing. | neg · low |
| **compunction** | A faint prick of conscience that gives one pause before or after a questionable act. | Slighter and more anticipatory than guilt or remorse; it is the small scruple that nags rather than the full weight of having done wrong. | neg · low |
| **embarrassment** | An acute self-conscious discomfort at having appeared foolish, exposed, or awkward before others. | More socially triggered and less morally serious than guilt or shame; it concerns looking bad in the moment rather than being bad at one's core. | neg · med |
| **chagrin** | A vexed mixture of disappointment and embarrassment at one's own failure or humiliation. | Adds a sting of irritated wounded pride that plain embarrassment lacks; it is embarrassment soured by frustration at oneself. | neg · med |
| **guilt** | The pained sense of having done something wrong or harmful for which one is responsible. | Targets a specific act ('I did a bad thing'), whereas shame indicts the whole self ('I am bad'); guilt motivates repair, shame motivates hiding. | neg · med |
| **moral regret** | A sorrowful wish that one had acted differently because the choice violated one's values. | More reflective and value-focused than raw guilt, and lacking the urgency of remorse; it laments the decision rather than agonizing over the harm done. | neg · med |
| **contrition** | A sincere, sorrowful acknowledgment of wrongdoing coupled with the wish to make amends. | More repentant and amends-oriented than guilt, and gentler than remorse's anguish; it leans toward humility and reconciliation rather than self-torment. | neg · med |
| **remorse** | A deep, gnawing distress over a wrong one has committed and its consequences. | More intense and lingering than guilt, biting at one repeatedly; it dwells on the harm caused rather than merely registering responsibility. | neg · high |
| **shame** | A painful feeling that one's whole self is flawed, defective, or unworthy in others' eyes. | Indicts the self rather than the act as guilt does, and is more global and identity-level than embarrassment's situational sting. | neg · high |
| **disgrace** | The felt collapse of honor and standing experienced upon a fall into public dishonor. | Centers on the felt loss of reputation and status before a community, whereas shame is the private inner experience of being unworthy. | neg · high |
| **humiliation** | The searing pain of being degraded and stripped of dignity, especially before others. | Typically imposed by others' contempt and felt as unjust lowering, whereas shame arises from one's own sense of defectiveness. | neg · high |
| **mortification** | An overwhelming, crushing shame so intense one feels as if one could die of it. | The most extreme acute point on this spectrum, far exceeding embarrassment or humiliation in the annihilating wish to vanish entirely. | neg · high |
| **self-loathing** | A profound, settled hatred and contempt directed at one's own self. | Chronic and totalizing where shame can be episodic, it crosses from feeling unworthy into actively despising oneself. | neg · high |
| **survivor's guilt** | Guilt for having been spared a misfortune that befell others. | Unlike ordinary guilt (over one's wrongdoing), it arises despite no fault, from comparative fortune. | neg · med |
| **shame spiral** | A self-amplifying cascade where shame about a flaw triggers shame about feeling shame. | A meta-emotional escalation, distinct from a single episode of shame or humiliation. | neg · high |
| **impostor feeling** | The fear of being exposed as a fraud despite real competence. | Blends shame and fear; unlike self-doubt it persists in the face of objective success. | neg · med |

## Pride & confidence — and its dark side

The self appraised well — and where it curdles into conceit.

| Word | What it is | What sets it apart | Valence · arousal |
|------|-----------|--------------------|-------------------|
| **self-respect** | A quiet, baseline regard for one's own worth and standards of conduct. | Unlike pride or accomplishment, it is not tied to any specific achievement but to a steady sense of one's inherent value; dignity is its outward, situational expression. | pos · low |
| **dignity** | The composed sense of one's own worth that keeps one's bearing intact before others or under adversity. | Where self-respect is the private feeling, dignity is its calm public form under pressure; it lacks the energized glow of pride. | pos · low |
| **gratification** | The mild pleasure of having a desire, need, or effort fulfilled. | Lighter and more sensory than satisfaction's appraisal of a job well done; it rewards a wish met rather than a standard achieved. | pos · low |
| **satisfaction** | The contented feeling that something done or attained meets one's standard. | More appraisal-driven and lower-key than accomplishment, which carries a fuller sense of completed effort; gratification is closer to simple pleasure. | pos · low |
| **confidence** | An assured belief that one can meet a particular challenge or that an outcome will go well. | Forward-looking and task-focused; unlike pride it points ahead to a challenge rather than back at an accomplishment. | pos · med |
| **accomplishment** | The warm, fulfilling sense that one has completed something demanding through one's own effort. | Fuller and more effortful than satisfaction, and more inward than pride, which adds the wish to be seen and esteemed for it. | pos · med |
| **pride** | The glow of pleasure and self-worth from one's own achievement, quality, or possession. | Adds a desire for recognition and elevated standing that accomplishment lacks; it is broader and more relational than the inward satisfaction of a finished task. | pos · med |
| **self-satisfaction** | A complacent pleasure in one's own qualities or achievements that has stopped reaching further. | Unlike honest pride it carries a faintly stale, undeserved-rest quality; smugness adds an outward, irritating display of it. | mixed · low |
| **smugness** | An offensively pleased self-regard openly displayed toward others. | More outward and grating than private self-satisfaction, and pettier than conceit, which is an inflated estimate of one's worth rather than a displayed glow. | neg · low |
| **vindication** | The charged relief and pride of being proven right or cleared after doubt or accusation. | Unlike triumph it is reactive and morally framed, vindicating prior judgment rather than celebrating a conquest; its satisfaction is sharpened by having been wronged. | mixed · high |
| **conceit** | An inflated, exaggerated opinion of one's own worth or abilities. | Where smugness is a displayed feeling, conceit is the distorted self-estimate behind it; it is more personal and less morally fated than hubris. | neg · med |
| **triumph** | The exultant, high-energy pride of decisive victory or success. | More intense and victory-bound than ordinary pride, and unlike vindication it celebrates winning rather than being proven right. | pos · high |
| **vainglory** | An empty, boastful pride that craves admiration for one's own glory. | Showier and more hollow than conceit, fixating on display and applause; it lacks the moral-overreach, doom-courting dimension of hubris. | neg · high |
| **hubris** | An overweening, dangerous pride that overreaches its proper bounds and courts a fall. | The most intense and self-destructive of the dark-side terms; unlike vainglory's craving for applause, it is a defiant overconfidence that invites comeuppance. | neg · high |
| **quiet pride** | Quiet self-regard in an achievement held without need to display or compare. | Distinct from smugness or vainglory; pride tempered by humility and free of one-upmanship. | pos · low |
| **self-efficacy** | The grounded belief that one can execute what a situation demands. | More specific and forward-looking than confidence; tied to capability for a particular task. | pos · med |
