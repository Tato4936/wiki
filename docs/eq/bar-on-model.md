# Bar-On model & the EQ-i

> **TL;DR.** Bar-On's model coined the term "EQ" and produced the first widely-used self-report inventory
> (the EQ-i). It's an "emotional-social intelligence" mixed model — historically important, but, like
> other self-report models, it overlaps personality and wellbeing more than it measures a distinct
> ability.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-03

## What it is

Reuven Bar-On framed **emotional-social intelligence**: the emotional and social competencies that shape
how effectively we understand and express ourselves, relate to others, and cope with daily demands. His
**EQ-i** self-report inventory organizes this into five composites:

- **Intrapersonal** (self-regard, emotional self-awareness, assertiveness)
- **Interpersonal** (empathy, relationships, social responsibility)
- **Stress management** (stress tolerance, impulse control)
- **Adaptability** (reality testing, flexibility, problem-solving)
- **General mood** (optimism, happiness)

It belongs to the **mixed / trait** family: self-reported, it overlaps personality and subjective
wellbeing, so it's weaker than the [ability model](mayer-salovey-ability-model.md) as a claim about a
separate intelligence. Its main significance is historical — popularizing "EQ" and giving practitioners
a usable instrument.

## How to apply

- Treat the EQ-i like the other self-report models: a **snapshot of self-perceived functioning and
  wellbeing**, good for reflection, not a performance measure of skill.
- The "general mood" composite makes the overlap obvious — high scores partly reflect *feeling good*, not
  just *being skilled*.
