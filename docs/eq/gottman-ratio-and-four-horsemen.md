# Gottman ratio & Four Horsemen

> **TL;DR.** Four conflict behaviors — criticism, contempt, defensiveness, stonewalling — reliably
> signal relationships in trouble (contempt most of all). The famous "5:1 ratio" and "90%+ divorce
> prediction" figures, however, are contested.

## What it is

From John Gottman's observational studies of couples, two ideas escaped into popular culture.

**The Four Horsemen** — conflict behaviors that predict relationship deterioration:

Criticism
:   Attacking the partner's character rather than a specific behavior.

Contempt
:   Disdain, mockery, superiority. The single strongest predictor of dissolution.

Defensiveness
:   Counter-attacking or playing the victim instead of taking responsibility.

Stonewalling
:   Withdrawing, shutting down, going silent.

**The "magic ratio"** — a claim that stable relationships maintain roughly **5 positive interactions
for every 1 negative** during conflict.

!!! warning "Contested — handle the numbers with care"
    The Four Horsemen, as *qualitative risk factors*, are well-supported and widely replicated —
    especially contempt. But the headline **numbers** are disputed:

    - The often-cited "**over 90% accuracy** predicting divorce" came from models that were **fit and
      tested on overlapping data** — fit the equation to a sample, then "predict" that same sample.
      That's retrodiction, not out-of-sample prediction; the figures don't generalize the way the
      slogan implies (Heyman & Slep, 2001).
    - The precise **5:1 ratio** is a memorable heuristic, not a robust universal constant.

    Use the Four Horsemen as a **diagnostic vocabulary**; treat the exact percentages and ratios as
    folklore.

## How to apply

- Watch for the Horsemen in your own conflicts — **contempt especially.** Its presence is the loudest
  signal; its absence is protective.
- The repair direction is concrete: swap criticism → specific complaint, contempt → appreciation,
  defensiveness → taking responsibility, stonewalling → a self-soothing break, then return.
- Internalize the *spirit* of the ratio — positives must heavily outweigh negatives — without trusting
  the exact 5:1.
