# Empathy: cognitive, affective, compassionate

> **TL;DR.** "Empathy" is at least three different things — *understanding* someone's feelings
> (cognitive), *sharing* them (affective), and *caring + wanting to help* (compassion). The distinction
> matters: affective empathy can burn you out, while compassion is trainable and protective.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-03

## What it is

Three dissociable components, often lumped together:

Cognitive empathy
:   Understanding another's state — perspective-taking, [theory of mind](theory-of-mind.md) applied to
    feelings.

Affective empathy
:   *Feeling* what the other feels — vicarious sharing of their emotion.

Compassion
:   A warm, other-oriented motivation to help, *without* necessarily mirroring their distress.

The most useful finding (Singer & Klimecki): **affective empathy can tip into "empathic distress"** — you
absorb the suffering, get overwhelmed, and withdraw (a route to caregiver burnout). **Compassion is
different** — it engages caring/approach systems, feels warm rather than depleting, and is **trainable**
(compassion meditation). So the goal usually isn't *more* affective empathy but the shift from empathic
distress to compassion.

## How to apply

- When helping someone in pain, aim for **compassion (warm, steady, helpful)** rather than drowning in
  **affective empathy (sharing the distress)** — the former lasts, the latter burns out.
- **Cognitive empathy** can be practiced deliberately (perspective-taking) even when affective empathy is
  low — useful for negotiation, leadership, and hard conversations.
