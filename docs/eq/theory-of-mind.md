# Theory of mind (mentalizing)

> **TL;DR.** Theory of mind is the ability to attribute mental states — beliefs, desires, intentions — to
> other people, and to grasp that theirs can differ from yours and from reality. It's the cognitive
> machinery underneath "reading people."

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-03

## What it is

**Theory of mind (ToM)**, or **mentalizing**, is representing others as having minds — beliefs, goals,
knowledge — that drive their behavior and may be *false* or simply different from your own. The classic
test is the **false-belief task**: Sally puts a marble in a basket and leaves; Anne moves it; where will
Sally look? Passing requires modeling Sally's *belief* rather than reality; children typically pass around
age four. The idea began with Premack & Woodruff's question of whether chimpanzees have it.

ToM is the cognitive substrate of **[cognitive empathy](empathy-types.md)**: you can't accurately read or
predict someone without modeling their separate mental state. Deficits are studied in autism (Baron-Cohen's
false-belief work).

## How to apply

- **Reading people well is mostly disciplined ToM**: explicitly ask "what does *this person* know, want,
  and believe — given *their* information, not mine?" The most common social error is the **curse of
  knowledge** — assuming others know what you know.
- Practicing perspective-taking (stating the other side's beliefs/goals in your own words) sharpens
  cognitive empathy regardless of how much you *feel* with them.
