# Goleman's mixed model

> **TL;DR.** Goleman's 1995 bestseller made "EQ" famous by bundling emotional *abilities* together with
> personality traits and motivation into five components. Great for popularizing the idea — but the broad
> model is psychometrically muddier than the ability model, and its headline "EQ matters more than IQ"
> claim is overstated.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-03

## What it is

Daniel Goleman's *Emotional Intelligence* (1995) is the reason "EQ" entered everyday language. His
"mixed" model — so called because it *mixes* abilities with traits and motives — has five components:

1. **Self-awareness** — recognizing your own emotions.
2. **Self-regulation** — managing them.
3. **Motivation** — drive, optimism, using emotion to pursue goals.
4. **Empathy** — reading others' emotions.
5. **Social skills** — managing relationships and influence.

(His later workplace work, 1998, reorganized these into four domains of "competencies.")

!!! warning "Popular, but watch the claims"
    Two cautions. First, **construct validity**: the mixed model folds in things that look a lot like
    standard personality traits (conscientiousness, optimism), so it overlaps heavily with the Big Five
    and is harder to defend as a *distinct* intelligence than the
    [ability model](mayer-salovey-ability-model.md). Second, the famous **"EQ predicts success more than
    IQ"** line is not well supported — incremental validity over IQ and personality is real but modest,
    not dominant.

## How to apply

- Use the five components as a **practical vocabulary** for what to work on — they map cleanly to
  trainable skills — without treating "EQ" as a magic predictor of success.
- When "EQ" is sold as a personality score or a success guarantee, that's this tradition; reach for the
  [ability model](mayer-salovey-ability-model.md) when you want rigor.
