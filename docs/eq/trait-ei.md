# Trait EI (Petrides)

> **TL;DR.** "Trait emotional intelligence" is your *self-perceived* emotional ability. Measured by
> self-report, it behaves like a personality trait (and overlaps the Big Five), not like a mental
> ability — so it answers a different question than the ability model.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-03

## What it is

Petrides and Furnham drew a sharp, useful line: **how you *perform* on emotional tasks (ability EI)** is
different from **how emotionally capable you *believe* you are (trait EI)**. Trait EI — "emotional
self-efficacy," measured by the TEIQue questionnaire — spans self-perceived wellbeing, self-control,
emotionality, and sociability.

The key empirical finding: trait EI **sits inside personality space**. It correlates strongly with the
Big Five (especially low Neuroticism, high Extraversion/Agreeableness), so it's best understood as a
*compound personality trait*, not a cognitive ability. That's not a flaw — it predicts real outcomes like
wellbeing and coping — but it's why "EQ tests" that are just questionnaires measure personality, not
skill.

## How to apply

- Know which you're measuring. A **self-report "EQ quiz" measures trait EI** (your self-image); a
  **performance test** like the [MSCEIT](msceit.md) targets ability EI. They can diverge — someone can
  rate themselves highly and still misread a room.
- Trait EI is useful for predicting wellbeing/coping; don't read it as proof of skill.
