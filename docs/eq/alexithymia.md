# Alexithymia

> **TL;DR.** Alexithymia is a trait of difficulty identifying and describing your own feelings, plus an
> externally-focused thinking style. It's essentially the low end of emotional awareness — a
> well-validated construct, not just "being unemotional."

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-03

## What it is

Coined by Sifneos (literally "no words for emotions"), **alexithymia** has three facets, captured by the
widely-used **Toronto Alexithymia Scale (TAS-20)**:

1. **Difficulty identifying feelings** — and telling feelings apart from bodily sensations.
2. **Difficulty describing feelings** to others.
3. **Externally-oriented thinking** — attention on external events over inner experience.

It's a **dimensional trait** (everyone sits somewhere on it), elevated in conditions like depression,
PTSD, and autism, but common in the general population too. Think of it as the deficit end of the
awareness skills this cluster is about: low alexithymia ≈ high
[emotional granularity](emotional-granularity.md).

## How to apply

- If naming feelings is consistently hard, that's a recognizable, *workable* profile — the remedy is the
  same skill-building as granularity: practicing specific labels and noticing the body–emotion link
  ([affect labeling](affect-labeling.md), [emotional granularity](emotional-granularity.md)).
- Don't read low expressiveness as not caring — alexithymia is about *access to and words for* emotion,
  not its absence.
