# Time-blocking

> **TL;DR.** Give **every hour of the workday a job** in advance, instead of working from a reactive to-do list. A task with a *when and where* gets done; a task floating on a list competes with whatever's loudest. Block your **deep work first**, fit the shallow stuff around it, and re-block when reality diverges — the plan is a tool, not a contract.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-08

## What it is

Newport's **time-block planning**: each morning (or the night before), divide the day into blocks and assign specific work to each. A to-do list tells you *what*; it doesn't defend *when*, so the day fills with the urgent and the easy. Time-blocking forces the scarce-resource decision up front and confronts you with how few deep hours actually exist.

It's the calendar-level twin of [implementation intentions](../habits/implementation-intentions.md): the evidence that **specific when/where plans** beat vague intentions is strong, and a time block is exactly that for your work. (Time-blocking *as a system* is practitioner advice — `medium` confidence — but it inherits that well-supported core.) Choose a **depth rhythm** to hang blocks on: *rhythmic* (a deep block at the same time daily) is the most durable; *bimodal* (deep days vs. admin days) if your schedule allows.

## How to apply

- **Block tomorrow tonight,** or first thing: deep work in your sharpest hours, comms/admin batched into a couple of windows.
- **Treat overruns as information,** not failure — re-block the rest of the day; the point is intentionality, not prophecy.
- **Protect one daily deep block** as non-negotiable (the rhythmic philosophy) before adding anything fancier.
- **Defend the blocks** by removing cues (→ [distraction & dopamine](distraction-and-dopamine.md)) and refusing to interleave (→ [attention residue](attention-residue.md)).
