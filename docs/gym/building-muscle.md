# Building muscle (hypertrophy)

> **TL;DR.** Muscle growth comes down to **enough volume, taken close enough to failure, progressed over
> time** — plus protein and recovery. Rep range is forgiving (anything ~5–30 reps works if you push hard);
> weekly *volume* per muscle is the bigger dial.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

The evidence (Schoenfeld and others) on hypertrophy:

- **Volume** — roughly **10–20 hard sets per muscle group per week** suits most people; more volume
  generally means more growth, up to a point of diminishing returns.
- **Effort (proximity to failure)** — sets should be taken **close to failure** (~0–3 reps in reserve). Sets
  miles from failure do little.
- **Rep range is flexible** — **~5 to ~30 reps** all build muscle if the set is genuinely hard. No magic
  number; pick ranges you can progress and recover from (often ~6–15 for most work).
- **Progression** — apply [progressive overload](consistency-and-progressive-overload.md): more reps/load
  over weeks.

Plus the support: **protein** ([nutrition](protein-and-nutrition.md)) and **recovery**
([recovery & sleep](recovery-and-sleep.md)) — muscle is built between sessions, not during them.

## How to apply

- Hit each muscle group **~2× per week**, accumulating ~10–20 hard sets weekly, most near failure.
- **Progress something** week to week (reps or load). If a lift stalls for weeks, check volume and recovery.
- Be patient: natural muscle gain is **slow** (a handful of kg in a *good* first year, less after). Distrust
  anyone promising fast.
