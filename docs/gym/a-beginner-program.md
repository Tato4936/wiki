# A beginner program

> **TL;DR.** You don't need anything fancy: **2–3 full-body sessions a week** built on the big compound
> movements (squat, hinge, push, pull), adding a little weight or a rep each week. Keep it simple,
> repeatable, and progress it. Fancy splits are for later.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

For a beginner, **full-body 2–3×/week** is ideal — it hits each muscle often, recovers easily, and forgives
missed days. A simple per-session template:

- A **squat / leg** movement (squat, leg press)
- A **hinge** (Romanian deadlift, deadlift)
- An **upper push** (bench press, overhead press, dips, push-ups)
- An **upper pull** (row, lat pulldown, pull-ups)
- Optional: a little direct arm / core / calf work

Do ~**2–4 sets** of each, mostly ~6–12 reps, stopping a rep or two short of failure, and **add load or a rep**
when you hit the top of your range. Warm up, train, log it, leave. Run it for months — that's the whole plan.

Off-the-shelf beginner routines (Starting Strength, StrongLifts 5×5, GZCLP, the r/Fitness Basic Beginner
Routine) all encode exactly this. Pick one and **run it** instead of program-hopping.

## How to apply

- **Pick one** program and commit ~3 months before judging it — program-hopping is the classic beginner
  mistake.
- **Log everything** and chase small weekly progress.
- Don't add volume or complexity until simple progression stops working.
