# Form & injuries

> **TL;DR.** Good technique + not ego-lifting + sensible progression keeps you training for *years* — which
> is what actually builds a body. The biggest mistake isn't "imperfect form," it's loading too heavy too
> soon and chasing numbers your tissues aren't ready for.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Injuries cost weeks or months — the enemy of [consistency](consistency-and-progressive-overload.md), which
is what makes progress. The fundamentals of staying healthy:

- **Learn the movements** before loading them — film yourself, use a coach or good tutorials, start light.
- **Don't ego-lift** — a weight you can't control (heaving, half-reps, form breakdown) feeds your ego and
  your physio. Leave a rep or two in reserve, especially early.
- **Progress gradually** — connective tissue adapts slower than enthusiasm; add load in small steps.
- **Warm up** with lighter sets, and **manage niggles early** rather than training through real pain.

Perspective: lifting with slightly imperfect form isn't automatically dangerous, and bodies are robust — but
*sudden big loads* on *unprepared tissue* with *runaway ego* is how people get hurt.

## How to apply

- Treat the first months as **skill acquisition**: lighter, controlled, learning the patterns. Strength
  follows.
- **Stay a little conservative** — the goal is to still be training in 5 years, because that consistency is
  what builds the physique.
