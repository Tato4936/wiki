# Getting stronger

> **TL;DR.** Strength is a skill *and* a tissue adaptation: train the **big compound lifts** in lower rep
> ranges with heavier loads, practice the technique, and add weight over time. For beginners, strength and
> muscle come from the same training — you don't have to choose.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Strength work emphasizes **heavier loads, lower reps** (often ~1–6 reps for top sets), longer rest, and the
**compound movements** that move the most weight and train the most muscle at once:

- **Squat** (knee-dominant) · **hinge/deadlift** (hip-dominant) · **press** (push — bench/overhead) ·
  **row/pull-up** (pull).

Strength is partly **neural skill** — your nervous system learning to recruit muscle efficiently — which is
why technique and consistency matter so much. For **beginners**, strength and
[muscle](building-muscle.md) arrive together; simple **linear progression** (adding a little weight each
session) works for months before you need anything fancier.

## How to apply

- Anchor training on the **big compounds**, and **add a small amount of weight** whenever you hit your target
  reps with good form — [progressive overload](consistency-and-progressive-overload.md) in practice.
- **Rest enough between heavy sets** (a few minutes); strength work isn't cardio.
- Prioritize **technique** early — the load you can handle *safely* is the skill.
