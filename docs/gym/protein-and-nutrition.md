# Protein & nutrition

> **TL;DR.** For building muscle, two things dominate: eat **enough protein** (~1.6–2.2 g per kg of
> bodyweight per day) and set **calories** to your goal (slight surplus to gain, moderate deficit to lean
> out). Training is the stimulus; food is the raw material.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

- **Protein** — a meta-analysis (Morton et al., 2018) puts the muscle-building sweet spot around
  **1.6 g/kg/day**, with little extra benefit beyond ~2.2 g/kg. Spread it across the day; any sufficient
  source works (whey is just convenient). It's the single most important diet variable for training results.
- **Calories** — body composition follows energy balance:
    - **Gain muscle:** a *small* surplus (~250–500 kcal/day over maintenance). Bigger surpluses just add fat.
    - **Lose fat:** a *moderate* deficit, kept high-protein and with training, to preserve muscle.
- **The rest** — enough carbs to fuel training, fats for health, mostly whole foods, enough fiber. Beyond
  protein and calories, details matter far less than people think.

## How to apply

- **Hit your protein first** (~1.6 g/kg) — it does more than any supplement.
- **Pick one goal at a time** (gain *or* lean) and set calories accordingly; chasing both at once stalls
  most people. Adjust by the scale/mirror over weeks.
- The one supplement worth it for most: **creatine monohydrate** (~3–5 g/day) — cheap, safe, well-evidenced
  for strength and size. Most others are noise.
