# What's actually attractive (the science)

> **TL;DR.** Across cultures, a few features reliably read as attractive: **averageness, symmetry, and
> health/youth cues** (clear skin, good condition). "Average" sounds like an insult but means *prototypical*
> — and most of these are partly within your control via health, grooming, and body composition.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

The research on facial/physical attractiveness (Rhodes' review; Langlois & Roggman) converges on a few
consistent drivers:

- **Averageness** — faces close to the mathematical *average* of the population are rated as more
  attractive. "Average" = prototypical and free of unusual features, not "mediocre." Composite faces beat
  almost all their constituents.
- **Symmetry** — bilateral symmetry is mildly attractive, plausibly a health/development cue.
- **Health & youth cues** — clear, even skin, good color, healthy weight/condition, clear eyes. These
  signal vitality, and they're the **most controllable** of the lot.
- **Secondary sex characteristics** — some sexual dimorphism, context- and preference-dependent.

The takeaway isn't "measure your canthal tilt." It's that a large share of attractiveness is **health and
condition**, which grooming, fitness, skin, and sleep directly move — see
[controllable vs. fixed](controllable-vs-fixed.md).

## How to apply

- Optimize the **health/condition cues** you control — [skin](skin/index.md),
  [body composition](body-composition.md), sleep, grooming — rather than fixating on fixed bone metrics.
- Treat "averageness/symmetry" as *already-mostly-fine for most people*; the variance you can actually
  affect is condition and presentation.
