# Retinoids

> **TL;DR.** Retinoids (vitamin-A derivatives — tretinoin, adapalene, retinol) are the **one skincare active
> with strong evidence** for both acne and anti-aging. If you add a single "active" beyond the
> [basics](skincare-basics.md), make it this — started low and slow.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Retinoids speed cell turnover and stimulate collagen. The evidence base is unusually solid for skincare:
**topical tretinoin is proven to reduce fine wrinkles and photoaging**, and retinoids (incl. OTC adapamene
and retinol) are first-line for **acne**. Strength ladder, weakest to strongest: retinol (OTC) →
adapalene (OTC) → tretinoin (prescription).

!!! note "Start low and slow"
    Retinoids irritate at first ("retinization") — redness, peeling, dryness — which is why people quit.
    Begin **2–3 nights a week** at a low strength, moisturize, and build up over weeks. They also **increase
    sun sensitivity**, so daily [sunscreen](sunscreen.md) is mandatory. Not while pregnant.

## How to apply

- Add **one** retinoid at night, low strength, **a few nights a week**, then ramp as your skin tolerates it.
  Patience beats potency.
- Always pair with **moisturizer** (buffers irritation) and **morning SPF** (non-negotiable with retinoids).
- For stubborn acne or if you want the strong stuff (tretinoin), see a **dermatologist**.
