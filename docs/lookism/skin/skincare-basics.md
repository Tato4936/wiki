# Skincare basics (the minimalist routine)

> **TL;DR.** Effective skincare is short: **cleanse, moisturize, and wear sunscreen** — plus a
> [retinoid](retinoids.md) if you want the one real anti-aging/acne active. The 10-step routines are mostly
> marketing; consistency with the basics beats a cabinet of serums.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Clear, healthy skin is one of the most reliable attractiveness and health cues
([what's attractive](../what-is-attractive.md)) — and the evidence-based core is small:

- **Sunscreen (AM)** — by far the highest-impact step; prevents photoaging and protects everything else.
  Its own note: [sunscreen](sunscreen.md).
- **Moisturizer** — keeps the skin barrier healthy; reduces dryness/irritation. A simple one is fine.
- **Gentle cleanser** — once or twice a day; avoid harsh, stripping, over-exfoliating routines that wreck
  the barrier.
- **A [retinoid](retinoids.md) (PM)** — the one active with strong evidence for both aging and acne. Start
  low and slow.

That's it for most people. Extra serums (vitamin C, niacinamide, exfoliating acids) are *optional* add-ons
with smaller, situational returns — not the foundation. **More products ≠ better skin**; the barrier-wrecking
12-step routine is a common own-goal.

## How to apply

- Build the habit around **three things: cleanse, moisturize, SPF** — daily, consistently. Add a retinoid
  at night once that's stable.
- **Introduce one new product at a time**, and give it weeks; piling on actives causes the irritation
  people then blame on "sensitive skin."
- Persistent acne, rosacea, or real concerns → see a **dermatologist**; it's worth it.
