# Sunscreen

> **TL;DR.** Daily sunscreen is the single most effective anti-aging skincare habit there is — a
> randomized trial showed regular use measurably **slows skin aging.** Most visible "aging" is sun damage;
> blocking it is the highest-ROI thing you can do for your skin, for decades.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

UV exposure is the dominant cause of **photoaging** — wrinkles, sagging, uneven tone, spots — on top of
skin-cancer risk. The standout evidence: in a randomized controlled trial (Hughes et al., 2013), adults
using **daily broad-spectrum sunscreen showed no detectable increase in skin aging** over ~4.5 years,
versus visible aging in the discretionary-use group. Few interventions in any field have that kind of
prevention data.

Practical points: use **broad-spectrum SPF 30+**, apply enough (most people use far too little), and
reapply if you're out in strong sun. Daily, year-round, even indoors-ish days — UVA passes through windows.

## How to apply

- Make **SPF the one non-negotiable** of your routine — every morning, rain or shine. It's worth more than
  every serum combined.
- Apply a proper amount (roughly a teaspoon for the face/neck); thin layers under-deliver the protection.
- Find one whose feel you like (so you'll actually wear it) — that matters more than chasing the "best"
  formula.
