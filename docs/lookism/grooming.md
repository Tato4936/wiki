# Grooming

> **TL;DR.** Grooming is the **highest-ROI, fastest** appearance lever there is — a good haircut, tidy
> facial hair and brows, clean nails, fresh breath, and not smelling bad move you more, per hour spent,
> than almost anything else. It's pure controllable, and most people leave gains on the table.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Grooming is the maintenance layer of attractiveness — and it's where neglected people make the biggest
jumps, because the baseline is often low. The high-impact items, roughly in order:

- **Haircut that fits your face/hair type** — get a *good* barber/stylist and actually maintain it; this
  is the single biggest grooming lever. A great cut > expensive everything else.
- **Facial hair** — either clean-shaven or deliberately groomed (lined up, trimmed), never patchy neglect.
- **Eyebrows** — tidy strays / unibrow; small effort, surprisingly large effect on the face.
- **Skin** — see the [skin subsection](skin/index.md); clear skin is a core attractiveness cue.
- **Teeth & breath** — clean, white-ish teeth and fresh breath (see [the basics](the-unsexy-basics.md)).
- **Nails & hands** — clean, trimmed.
- **Smell** — daily shower, deodorant, and a *light* signature scent. Smelling bad nukes everything else.

The research backdrop: much of measured "attractiveness" is **groomed** attractiveness, not raw structure —
which is exactly why this is high leverage (see [controllable vs. fixed](controllable-vs-fixed.md)).

## How to apply

- Knock out the **baseline** first (haircut, brows, skin, smell, teeth) before chasing anything advanced —
  it's the cheapest, biggest win.
- Treat grooming as **maintenance, not a one-off**: a great cut grows out in 3–4 weeks. Put it on a schedule.
