# Style & fit

> **TL;DR.** **Fit beats everything** — clothes that actually fit your body look better than expensive
> clothes that don't. Most people's "I don't dress well" problem is really a *fit* problem, and it's
> cheap to fix (tailoring, the right sizes, simple well-fitting basics).

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Clothing changes perceived body shape, status, and put-togetherness — and the dominant variable isn't price
or trend, it's **fit**. A $30 shirt that fits the shoulders and tapers right reads better than a $200 one
that's a tent. The hierarchy of what matters:

1. **Fit** — shoulders sit right, sleeves/hem the right length, not boxy, not squeezed. Off-the-rack rarely
   fits perfectly; a cheap **tailor** (taking in a shirt, hemming pants) is the highest-ROI move in your
   closet.
2. **Fundamentals over flash** — well-fitting plain basics in good condition beat loud, ill-fitting pieces.
   Build a simple, coherent base first.
3. **Color & contrast** — colors that suit your skin tone; not too many at once.
4. **Condition** — clean, unwrinkled, no worn-out shoes (people read shoes).

This compounds with [body composition](body-composition.md): clothes hang well on a body that's in decent
shape, and fit problems shrink as your proportions improve.

## How to apply

- **Audit for fit, not quantity.** Try things on honestly (shoulders + length), and take your best pieces to
  a tailor. You'll look better in 5 well-fitting items than 30 random ones.
- Start with a small **uniform** of fitting basics you can repeat; expand only once the base is solid.
