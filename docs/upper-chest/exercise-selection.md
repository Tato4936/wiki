# Exercise selection

> **TL;DR.** Bias the upper chest by matching the **line of pull** to the clavicular fibers — resistance that drives your arms **up and toward the midline.** The staples: an **incline press** (~30°, barbell or dumbbell) as the heavy driver, plus a **low-to-high cable fly/press** for tension in the shortened position. Dumbbells allow a deeper stretch and a converging path; cables keep load on the pec where a free-weight fly goes slack.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-08

## What it is

Two things decide upper-pec emphasis: the **[angle](incline-angle.md)** and the **resistance vector**. The clavicular fibers pull the arm up-and-in, so the most direct loading is a movement resisting exactly that:

- **Incline barbell/dumbbell press (~30°)** — the heavy compound driver; [EMG-favored for the upper pec](incline-angle.md). Dumbbells add range and let the hands converge.
- **Low-to-high cable fly / cable press** — handles travel from hips to upper-chest height, so the line of pull is up-and-in *and* tension stays high at the top (the shortened position), where a flat dumbbell fly loses almost all resistance to gravity. A good complement to pressing.

⚠️ **Reverse-grip (supinated) bench press** is a popular "upper-chest" pick; the supporting EMG evidence is **limited and mixed**, and it's awkward to load safely — treat it as an optional experiment, not a staple.

## How to apply

- **Pair one press + one cable/fly:** e.g. incline dumbbell press (heavy, 6–12) **then** low-to-high cable fly (12–20, squeeze at the top).
- **Drive the arms up-and-in,** not straight out — the converging path is the point.
- **Use a full range:** stretch at the bottom, finish in the shortened position under tension.
- **Cables or a slight converging dumbbell path** beat a flat barbell for keeping tension on the upper pec at lockout.
