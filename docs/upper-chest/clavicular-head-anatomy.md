# The clavicular head & regional hypertrophy

> **TL;DR.** The pectoralis major has two heads — the **clavicular (upper)** and the larger **sternocostal (lower/mid)** — with different fiber directions, and they can be **recruited and grown somewhat independently**. That's why "training the upper chest" is legitimate rather than bro-science. But the heads always share the load, so what you're doing is a **bias, not isolation.**

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-08

## What it is

The pec major fans onto the upper arm from two origins: the **clavicular head** (from the collarbone) and the **sternocostal head** (from the sternum and ribs). Crucially, the clavicular fibers run **down-and-out** to the humerus — so they're best loaded by moving the arm **up and toward the midline** (shoulder flexion + horizontal adduction), the exact motion of an incline press or a low-to-high fly.

**Regional (non-uniform) hypertrophy** is the load-bearing fact: a muscle doesn't have to grow evenly, and exercise/angle selection can shift *where* it grows. Antonio (2000) documented non-uniform responses within muscles to resistance training, and later work has shown different exercises and joint angles bias growth toward different regions of the same muscle — including the pec. So the upper pec being under-developed is usually a **training-selection** problem (too much flat work), not your genetics being fixed.

The caveat: the two heads are one muscle and co-contract. You can tilt emphasis toward the clavicular head; you cannot switch the sternal head off.

## How to apply

- **Pick movements whose resistance opposes the clavicular fibers:** arm travelling **up and in** — incline presses, low-to-high cables (→ [exercise selection](exercise-selection.md)).
- **Don't expect isolation** — expect a bias you exploit consistently over months.
- **Treat the upper pec as its own priority** within chest training (→ [programming](programming.md)).
