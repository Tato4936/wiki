# Trusts & estate planning

> **TL;DR.** Trusts let the wealthy move assets to heirs while minimizing estate/gift tax, avoiding
> probate, controlling how money is used, and shielding it from creditors. They're the main machinery for
> passing fortunes across generations with the least leakage.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

A **trust** holds assets, managed by a **trustee** for **beneficiaries**, under rules the **grantor** sets.
Used for:

- **Avoiding probate** — trust assets pass outside the slow, public probate process.
- **Estate/gift-tax minimization** — moving appreciation out of your taxable estate.
- **Control** — dictating when and how heirs receive money (ages, conditions).
- **Asset protection** — shielding assets from future creditors or divorces.

A few of the famous vehicles:

- **GRAT** (grantor retained annuity trust) — put assets in, take back an annuity; **future appreciation
  passes to heirs essentially gift-tax-free.** Beloved for volatile, high-growth assets.
- **Irrevocable life insurance trust (ILIT)** — keeps life-insurance payouts out of the taxable estate.
- **Dynasty trust** — passes wealth across many generations, minimizing transfer tax at each one.
- **Charitable remainder trust** — income now plus a deduction, remainder to charity later.

All legal, all advisor-built, all jurisdiction-specific. The point is moving *appreciation* and *control*
out of the taxable estate while you're alive.

## How to apply

- The general principle: **transferring assets *before* they appreciate** (or freezing their estate value
  via a GRAT) moves the growth to the next generation outside the estate tax.
- Interacts with [stepped-up basis](stepped-up-basis.md) in subtle ways (gifted assets carry over basis;
  inherited assets step up) — exactly why this needs a real estate-planning professional.
