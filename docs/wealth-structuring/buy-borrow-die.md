# Buy, borrow, die

> **TL;DR.** The flagship way the ultra-wealthy fund a lavish life while reporting little taxable income:
> **buy** assets that appreciate, **borrow** against them instead of selling (loans aren't income, so no
> capital-gains tax), and **die** — at which point heirs get a [stepped-up basis](stepped-up-basis.md) that
> erases the lifetime gains. Legal.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

Three steps:

1. **Buy / hold.** Own appreciating assets — stock, real estate, business equity. As long as you don't
   *sell*, the gains are "unrealized" and **not taxed**. You can hold for decades.
2. **Borrow.** Need cash to live? Instead of selling (which triggers capital-gains tax), **borrow against
   the assets** — securities-backed lines of credit, margin, mortgages, often at low rates. **Loan proceeds
   are not taxable income.** You fund your lifestyle with cheap debt and report little income.
3. **Die.** At death, heirs inherit with a [**stepped-up basis**](stepped-up-basis.md) — the cost basis
   resets to market value, so the lifetime appreciation **escapes income tax entirely**. The loans are
   repaid from the estate.

ProPublica's "Secret IRS Files" showed several billionaires paid a "true tax rate" of a few percent — some
$0 in certain years — largely via this pattern. It's legal [avoidance](avoidance-vs-evasion.md), not evasion.

!!! note "The catches"
    It's not free money: the **estate tax** (US ~40% above the exemption) can claw back a lot at the "die"
    step; you pay **loan interest**; it requires a large, appreciating, borrowable asset base; and it's
    **US-centric** (it leans on stepped-up basis — other countries differ). The income-tax avoidance is
    real; "totally tax-free" is overstated.

## How to apply

- The portable lesson even at smaller scale: **realizing a gain is a *choice* that triggers tax** —
  holding, borrowing, and timing realizations are the levers. Selling assets to fund a purchase is often
  the *most*-taxed way to do it.
- It only fully "works" with serious assets and advisors; treat it as *how the top end works*, not a retail
  playbook.
