# Business expenses & the line

> **TL;DR.** Owning a business lets you pay for *genuine business costs* with **pre-tax** dollars —
> deducting "ordinary and necessary" expenses lowers taxable income. That's legal and a real advantage.
> Running *personal* spending through the company to deduct it (报销-ing personal stuff) is **tax fraud** —
> the line is whether the expense is truly for the business.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

A business is taxed on **profit** (revenue minus expenses), so every dollar of legitimate **"ordinary and
necessary" business expense** (US: IRC §162) reduces taxable income — letting the owner buy business things
with pre-tax money, while an employee buys everything with after-tax money. That asymmetry is the real,
legal advantage of ownership.

**Legitimately deductible** (when genuinely business-related and documented): business travel and lodging;
business meals (US: 50%); a **home office** used *regularly and exclusively* for the business; a vehicle
**to the extent of documented business use**; equipment, software, professional development, salaries.

!!! warning "Where it becomes evasion (报销 of personal spending)"
    Deducting **genuinely personal** expenses as business — a family vacation dressed as a "conference," a
    personal car, groceries, your kid's tuition — is **tax evasion**, not avoidance. It's a crime (back
    taxes, penalties, interest, possible prosecution), a classic audit trigger, and — if it isn't *your*
    company — can also be embezzlement. The cleverness of the label doesn't matter; the **facts** do (see
    [avoidance vs. evasion](avoidance-vs-evasion.md)). This note explains the *line*, not how to cross it.

The grey zone is real (a half-business meal, a trip with a personal day attached), which is why the rules
turn on **documentation and primary purpose** — and why careful owners keep records, not better stories.

## How to apply

- The legal play: **if you own a business, route *real* business costs through it and document them** — it
  genuinely lowers your effective cost of those things.
- The test for any expense: *would this exist if not for the business, and can I document its business
  purpose?* If it's personal, deducting it is fraud, full stop.

## Sources

1. IRC §162 (trade or business expenses — "ordinary and necessary"); §262 (personal, living, and family expenses are **not** deductible).
