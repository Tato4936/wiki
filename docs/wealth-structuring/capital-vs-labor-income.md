# Capital vs. labor income

> **TL;DR.** Money earned from *owning* (long-term capital gains, qualified dividends) is taxed at lower
> rates than money earned from *working* (wages). That structural gap is the simplest reason the wealthy,
> who live off capital, pay lower effective rates than high earners who live off salary.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** high · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

In the US, **long-term capital gains** (assets held >1 year) and **qualified dividends** are taxed at
preferential rates — **0% / 15% / 20%** (+3.8% net investment income tax) — while **ordinary income**
(wages, salary) is taxed progressively up to **37%**, *plus* payroll taxes that capital income doesn't pay.

So a worker earning $500k of salary can face a much higher effective rate than someone with $500k of
long-term capital gains. This is the engine behind Warren Buffett's line that he's taxed at a lower rate
than his secretary: his income is **capital**, hers is **labor**.

The wealthy's playbook flows from this: convert labor-like income into capital-like income wherever the law
allows (founder equity, [carried interest](carried-interest.md), holding for the long-term rate) and live
off capital rather than wages.

## How to apply

- The general lever: **how income is *characterized* matters as much as how much you earn.** Long-term
  capital gains < short-term gains < ordinary income, tax-wise.
- Even modestly: holding investments **past one year** to get the long-term rate, and favoring
  capital-gain / qualified-dividend income, is the retail version of the same idea.
