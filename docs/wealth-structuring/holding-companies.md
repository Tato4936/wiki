# Holding companies

> **TL;DR.** Wealthy families and owners rarely hold assets in their own name — they put businesses, real
> estate, and investments inside **holding companies** (LLCs, corporations). The reasons: liability
> protection, tax efficiency, cleaner wealth transfer, and privacy.

**Provenance:** `cited` · **Confidence:** medium · **Updated:** 2026-06-04

## What it is

A **holding company** owns stakes in other entities ("operating companies") and assets, rather than trading
itself. Stack a few and you get a family holdco owning LLCs that each own a business or property. Why:

- **Liability separation.** Each asset/business sits in its own entity, so a lawsuit or debt against one
  doesn't reach the others or the owner personally (the "corporate veil").
- **Tax efficiency.** Deferring tax by retaining earnings, moving dividends between related companies
  (often partly tax-free), and splitting income across entities or family members in lower brackets.
- **Estate / succession.** It's far easier to gift or bequeath **shares of a holdco** than to carve up the
  underlying businesses and properties — and minority shares can be **discounted** for gift-tax purposes.
- **Privacy.** Ownership sits behind the entity rather than the individual's name.

All standard and legal. The complexity is the point — and the cost (legal, accounting, compliance), which
is why it pays off mainly at real scale.

## How to apply

- The general idea even for a small operator: **separating assets into entities** limits liability and
  creates planning flexibility — but only if you respect the formalities (separate accounts, no
  commingling), or a court can "pierce the veil."
- Real structuring is jurisdiction-specific and advisor-driven; this is the *why*, not a DIY blueprint.
